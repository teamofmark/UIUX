    New Zealand – Taranaki Deep-Sea Mining Blocked
    [문단 - 01]
    The approval for a deep-sea mining project being pursued in the waters off Taranaki, New Zealand, has been finally rejected.
    Experts involved in the review determined that the project could cause serious damage to the marine ecosystem and would be difficult to operate safely, even with conditions attached.
    [문단 - 02]
    Significant concerns regarding the impact on protected marine life, such as fin whales and penguins, were cited as a major reason for the decision.
    Greenpeace, local communities, and citizens have opposed this plan for over a decade, with more than 60,000 New Zealanders joining their efforts. This decision is the result of those long-standing efforts, serving as a clear example of the dangers of deep-sea mining and marking a significant turning point in protecting the oceans.

Guinea-Bissau – A Choice for Marine Ecosystems
    [문단 - 01]
    Guinea-Bissau has made a choice to prioritize food security and marine ecosystems over industrial profits.
    The government of Guinea-Bissau implemented a complete ban on the production of fishmeal and fish oil starting January 2026. Fishmeal and fish oil are primarily used as feed for aquaculture.
    Factory fishing vessels and onshore processing facilities, as well as new fishing permits for these operations, have been suspended. Furthermore, the supply of catches from small fishing vessels to these factories has been prohibited, with penalties for violations. This marks an official acknowledgment that this industry causes food shortages and damages the environment and ecosystems.
    This measure is the result of long-standing efforts by local communities and civil society and will serve as an important policy precedent for West Africa as a whole.
    [문단 - 02]
    Fishmeal and fish oil factories in West Africa, including Guinea-Bissau, are threatening the food and livelihoods of up to 40 million people. These factories are taking large quantities of fresh fish meant for local residents to use as feed for aquaculture, pigs and chickens, and even pet food in Europe and Asia. Greenpeace has been campaigning alongside citizens to protect food security and ecosystems.

Netherlands – A Ruling for Climate-Vulnerable Groups
    [문단 - 01]
    A Dutch court has issued a significant ruling regarding the climate crisis. The court determined that the Dutch government violated the European Convention on Human Rights by not only failing to adequately protect the residents of Bonaire, a special autonomous region in the Caribbean, from the impacts of the climate crisis but also by discriminating against them. Greenpeace Netherlands filed a lawsuit in court in 2024 alongside the residents of Bonaire, demanding fair climate policies from the government, and has continued the litigation for two years.
    [문단 - 02]
    The court ordered the government to establish new, more equitable climate goals and provide substantial protection to residents from climate damages such as heatwaves and sea-level rise. It also emphasized the need to respond to the climate crisis in a manner commensurate with responsibility by setting a goal to limit the rise in global average temperature to within 1.5 degrees Celsius.
    Consequently, the Dutch government must reduce greenhouse gas emissions much faster than currently do.
    This ruling is a historic victory achieved by Greenpeace and the citizens!
