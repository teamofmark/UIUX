[Statement] ISA Puts Brakes on Deep-Sea Mining… Adoption of Mining Regulations Fails
[작성자 부분]
Text by: Greenpeace

Greenpeace: “Korean Government Must Support TMC Investigation and Call for Deep-Sea Mining Moratorium”

[문단 - 01]
(March 23, 2026) The 31st meeting of the International Seabed Authority (ISA) concluded on the 20th (Jamaica local time). The main point of contention at the meeting, which began on the 9th, was whether to adopt a Mining Code; however, as the majority of member states prioritized multilateral ocean governance, the Mining Code was not adopted. Approval for deep-sea mining was also not granted.

[문단 - 02]
At this meeting, member states stated that a rigorous investigation is needed into the possibility of contract violations by The Metals Company (TMC), which attempted to unilaterally push forward with deep-sea mining in coordination with the U.S. Trump administration. South Korea is also an ISA member and  has served <a href="https://www.mof.go.kr/doc/ko/selectDoc.do?docSeq=46918&searchDeptName=&menuSeq=971&searchEndDate=&searchEtc1=&searchEtc2=&searchEtc3=&searchEtc4=&searchEtc5=&currentPageNo=1&searchSelect=title&searchStartDate=&recordCountPerPage=&bbsSeq=10&searchValu=#:~:text=2022.08.05.&text=%ED%95%B4%EC%96%91%EC%88%98%EC%82%B0%EB%B6%80(%EC%9E%A5%EA%B4%80%20%EC%A1%B0%EC%8A%B9%ED%99%98)%EB%8A%94,%EC%9C%BC%EB%A1%9C%20%EC%9E%AC%EC%84%A0%EC%B6%9C%20%EB%90%98%EC%97%88%EB%8B%A4%EA%B3%A0%20%EB%B0%9D%ED%98%94%EB%8B%A4." target="_blank">as a board member</a>  in the Major Investor Group since 2009. The ISA is an international organization established under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea that oversees the development and management of deep-sea resources on the high seas.

[문단 - 03]
Previously, The Metals Company sparked controversy by attempting to secure independent deep-sea mining rights through cooperation with the Trump administration without formal approval from the ISA. The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) defines deep-sea resources as the common property of humanity and stipulates that deep-sea mining and resource development must undergo a multilateral approval process through the ISA.

[문단 - 04]
Accordingly, an official investigation into The Metals Company began last July, and the ISA Legal and Technical Commission (LTC) stated that at least one contractor may have violated ISA exploration contracts and related regulations. It also re-emphasized that governments have a responsibility to supervise and manage companies to ensure compliance with international law.

[문단 - 05]
At this meeting, the extension of investigations into companies suspected of contract violations was approved. While some companies argued that the investigation itself was illegal, this claim was not accepted; instead, calls were raised for more robust and comprehensive investigations. Countries such as Germany, the Netherlands, and Portugal pointed out that relying solely on companies' self-reporting is insufficient and urged the collection of broad evidence, including public data.

[문단 - 06]
Louisa Casson, a Greenpeace International campaigner who attended the meeting, stated, “While some companies are disregarding international law and applying pressure by even threatening legal action, member states have made it clear that they will not yield.” She added, “The Metals Company has also acknowledged in its own financial report that the suspension or termination of the ISA exploration contract could pose a significant risk to its business. If a breach of contract is confirmed, preparations must be made at the upcoming ISA meeting in July to actually take measures to suspend or terminate the contract.”

[문단 - 07]
He also added, “Delegations from around the world must unite against the Trump administration and The Metals Company’s neocolonial attempts to secure resources,” stating that it is “a critical moment to gauge how the international community will respond to attempts to divert the deep sea, a public asset, for private gain.”

[문단 - 08]
Kim Yeon-ha, a Greenpeace ocean campaigner, said, “As the host country of the next UN Ocean Conference, the Korean government must demonstrate clear ocean policy leadership for the oceans and future generations at the ISA meeting this coming July,” adding, “It must support a rigorous investigation into the alleged breach of contract by The Metals Company and clearly oppose rushing the adoption of mining regulations without sufficient scientific review.”

[문단 - 09]
Campaigner Kim went on to emphasize that the South Korean government should also join the moratorium on deep-sea mining. Sweden, Portugal, France, and others that hosted the UN General Assembly before South Korea have already officially supported the moratorium, and currently, <a href="https://deep-sea-conservation.org/solutions/no-deep-sea-mining/momentum-for-a-moratorium/" target="_blank"> a total of 40 countries have joined.</a>

[문단 - 10]
Greenpeace has consistently pointed out that rushing to enact mining regulations could serve only the interests of a few multinational corporations. Amid growing scientific, environmental, and social concerns surrounding deep-sea mining, what is needed now is not to rush the adoption of regulations, but to join a moratorium. Participating in a moratorium is the responsible choice necessary to protect the oceans, which are a common asset of humanity.
