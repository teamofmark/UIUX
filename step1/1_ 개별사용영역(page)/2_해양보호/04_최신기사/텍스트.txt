[부제 - 02]
What is the significance of the official entry into force of the 'Global Treaty on the Ocean (BBNJ)'?
    [문단 - 01]
    The entry into force of this treaty signifies the establishment of a powerful legal fence for the 'High Seas' for the first time in human history, which have long been left abandoned as ownerless waters. Although the High Seas are vast, covering more than half of the Earth's surface, the lack of a comprehensive legal framework for their management has left them defenseless against indiscriminate fishing and destructive development.

    [문단 - 02]
    However, countries that have ratified the treaty must now establish domestic laws and administrative systems for the designation of Marine Protected Areas (Marine Protected Areas), environmental impact assessments, and genetic resource management on the high seas, and must also participate in international consultations and implementation discussions, such as the upcoming Ocean COP. This is considered the most significant environmental advancement achieved by humanity since the Paris Climate Agreement.

    [문단 - 03]
    Global ocean treaties go beyond simply protecting the seas. They will serve as a decisive turning point that not only slows the climate crisis and restores disappearing marine biodiversity but also safeguards the food security of billions of people who depend on the ocean for their livelihoods.

[부제 - 03]
Why are you emphasizing that the '5-year countdown' has started now?
    [문단 - 01]
    This is because we have very little time left to fulfill our promise to designate 30% of the world's oceans as protected areas by 2030. Currently, only 0.9% of the open seas are properly protected.

    [문단 - 02]
    To increase this area by 30% within the next five years, we must designate protected areas faster and more extensively than any conservation effort in human history. This is a massive challenge, effectively equivalent to creating a protected area larger than a continent every month.

    [문단 - 03]
    The reason Greenpeace is calling for a '5-year countdown' is rooted in the desperation that if we do not act now, we could forever miss the golden opportunity to prevent the collapse of the marine ecosystem.
