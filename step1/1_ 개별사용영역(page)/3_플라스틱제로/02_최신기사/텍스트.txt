Press Release Plastic
 5 minutes June 22, 2026
[Report] Ready to graduate from plastic… 8 out of 10 citizens say “I want to reduce plastic”
[작성자 부분]
Text by: Greenpeace

[비 순차 목록 - 01]
    Plant Root Solidarity Announces Results of Plastic Pollution Awareness Survey Targeting 1,000 Adults Nationwide

    68.7% “Most products are made of single-use plastic, so we have no choice but to use them”

    Packaging, consumer goods, and textiles are cited as the industries most urgent for reduction. 

[문단 - 01]
(June 22, 2026) A survey revealed that eight out of ten citizens are willing to reduce their use of single-use plastics but are facing difficulties due to a lack of systems. Citizens called for the government and companies to play a role in moving away from plastic.

[문단 - 02]
The 'Alliance to Eradicate Plastic Problems' (hereinafter 'Plastic Roots Alliance'), composed of 17 domestic and international civic groups, held a 'Plastic Graduation Ceremony' in front of the Cheongwadae Sarangchae on the morning of the 22nd and announced the results of a 'Plastic Pollution Awareness Survey' conducted on 1,000 people. According to the survey results, 83.6% of respondents perceived the problem of plastic pollution as serious, and 81.3% answered that they were willing to reduce their use of plastic in daily life. The percentage of respondents who felt anxious about the detection of microplastics in the human body also reached 81.7%. 

[문단 - 03]
Supply chain crises caused by the war in the Middle East also appear to have influenced public awareness regarding moving away from plastic. 88.0% of respondents were aware of the disruptions in naphtha supply, and 71.9% stated that this led them to take a greater interest in moving away from plastic. 76.6% of respondents expressed concern about the impact of the naphtha shortage on their daily lives. 

[문단 - 04]
As for the reasons why they have no choice but to continue using plastic despite taking the plastic problem seriously (1st and 2nd place percentile), 68.7% of citizens cited "because most products they intend to purchase are sold exclusively in single-use plastic packaging." This was followed by 39.0% who responded that the purchasing/use process becomes more complicated if single-use plastics are not used. 

[문단 - 05]
In fact, citizens overwhelmingly agreed (79.1%) that the use of single-use plastics would decrease if reusable containers or refill systems were established, and 56.1% responded that they would reduce use if fees were imposed on plastic products or single-use delivery containers. Only 37.7% responded that they would continue to use plastics for daily convenience. 

[문단 - 06]
In response, the Grassroots Solidarity pointed out that “this indicates that the high plastic usage rate is a problem of market structure rather than individual will,” and noted that “improvements are needed in the process of citizens purchasing and using products, and if reusable containers or refill systems are established, single-use plastics could be drastically reduced.”

[문단 - 07]
Citizens responded that the government and corporations should lead the move away from plastic. When asked about the agents capable of driving change, 64.4% of respondents (1st and 2nd priority) answered "government," followed by 58.8% who answered "corporations." With 48.5% answering "individual consumers," this confirms the public perception that the government and corporations should lead the plastic-free movement. 

[문단 - 08]
The highest percentage of responses (1st and 2nd priority) regarding the most needed policy was the establishment of reusable containers and refill systems, at 44.4%, followed by policies that induce changes in consumer behavior, such as tumbler discounts or deposit systems, at 41.6%. Strengthening the environmental responsibility of manufacturing companies was also cited at 34.5%. 

[문단 - 09]
Awareness of reusable containers and refill systems was very high at 90.9%, while actual usage experience reached 55.0%. Regarding experience, "experience using reusable containers due to the ban on single-use items in stores" was 77.8%, whereas usage experience was low in places lacking policies or systems, such as baseball stadiums (21.1%), funeral homes (22%), and food delivery services (28.8%). This indicates a difference of approximately three times in the experience of using reusable containers between spaces where mandatory use is enforced and those where it is not. 

[문단 - 10]
Among the eight sectors—packaging, consumer goods, textiles, construction and building, industrial machinery, electrical and electronics, medical, and transportation—packaging was ranked first, consumer goods second, and textiles third as the industries most urgent for plastic reduction. On the other hand, the industries where plastic use is essential were found to be medical, electrical and electronics, and construction and building, in that order. 

[문단 - 11]
The Grassroots Solidarity stated, “Through this survey, we confirmed that citizens are already determined to part ways with single-use plastics,” and emphasized, “Urgent policy decisions from the government and corporations are needed to improve the plastic-centric production and distribution system that hinders individual will and action. The government must not further delay the introduction of regulations on the grounds of a lack of voluntary culture, public-private collaboration, or citizen participation.” Following this, they held a performance of throwing graduation caps to symbolize “graduation from a plastic society.” 

[문단 - 12]
Meanwhile, this survey is the result of an online survey on plastic usage awareness conducted by Macromill Embrain Co., Ltd. from the 27th to the 28th of last month, targeting 1,000 adult men and women aged 20 to 69 nationwide.
