Microplastics in squeeze baby food
[작성자 부분]
Text: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
Greenpeace has published a report on microplastics found in Nestlé and Danone's squeeze baby food and chemicals leaching from packaging. Is squeeze baby food truly a safe and healthy food option? Is it merely convenient?

[문단 - 01]
Raising a child inevitably makes one sensitive to food. Parents have endless concerns, such as whether the ingredients are organic, if the sodium content is appropriate, or if it is not too sweet. Furthermore, when going out with your child, it is difficult to give up the convenience of being able to feed them easily. For these reasons, many parents choose squeeze-type baby food, but at the same time, you may have also wondered, "Is it really safe?"

[부제 - 01]
Mom, I can see microplastics! (feat. microscope)
    [문단 - 01]
    Greenpeace International's new report, "Tiny Plastics, Big Problem: The Hidden Health Risks of Plastic Pouches for Baby Food," conducted an in-depth investigation into how much babies are exposed to microplastics through squeeze-type baby food. 
    [문단 - 02]
    To ensure accurate and objective research, we commissioned a specialized research institute to conduct an investigation, and the subjects of the study were two representative products from global brands: <b>Nestlé's Gerber yogurt-based puree</b> and <b>Danone's Happy Baby Organics fruit puree</b>. As a result of the experiment, large amounts of microplastics were detected in both products.

    <b>Per 1g of baby food (weight of a small raisin):</b> Gerber pouches average up to <b>54</b> , Happy Baby Organics pouches average up to <b>99</b> detected

    <b>Based on a teaspoonful:</b> Gerber up to <b>270 pieces</b> , Happy Baby Organics up to <b>495 pieces</b>

    <b>Based on an entire pack of baby food pouches: Gerber contains over 5,000 plastic particles</b>per pouch , and Happy Baby Organics contains <b>over 11,000</b> plastic particles.

    [문단 - 03]
    Where on earth did these microplastics come from? Investigation results confirmed a deep link between the detected microplastics and the polyethylene, the plastic material used for the pouch lining.

    [문단 - 04]
    Furthermore, various chemicals were detected in both the packaging and the food, and even the widely known endocrine disruptors were found in Gerber yogurt products. Gerber and Happy Baby Organics pouches are products that parents trust and purchase at major supermarkets and online stores worldwide. However, no one could have imagined that they contained so much microplastic. Due to the lack of alternatives to plastic, our children are left defenseless against danger.

 

The process by which microplastics penetrate our bodies
It is already well known that easily produced and discarded plastic packaging pollutes the Earth and exacerbates biodiversity loss and the climate crisis. Plastic packaging, which accounts for approximately 40% of global plastic production and waste, is already becoming a heavy burden on the planet.

Plastic pollution has now extended beyond the Earth and infiltrated our loved ones and even our bodies. Scientific research is proving that single-use plastics pose a serious threat to health. This Greenpeace report investigated not only microplastics but also the leakage of hazardous chemicals. As a result, 81 chemicals were provisionally identified in Danone's fruit purees and 111 in Nestlé's dairy-based purees, and these same substances were detected in the packaging materials of the products.



Due to the current lack of strong and proactive government regulations, companies are indiscriminately using plastic packaging for their own convenience and profit. These easily produced single-use plastics eventually break down into microplastics and spread across the globe. They then travel along the food chain, entering our bodies through air, water, and food, or, as in the case of this pouch product, through direct ingestion. The only way to escape the plastic crisis surrounding us is to reduce plastic packaging itself .

 

Plastic pollution is now a health issue for 'me and my child'
Nestlé and Danone, the companies investigated this time , account for a staggering 40% of the global baby food market and produce over 1 million tons of plastic packaging annually. The results of this irresponsible production are mostly discarded, polluting the Earth.

Why are companies able to produce plastic so unregulated? Korea is no exception. Plastic is being produced endlessly because policies focus on processing already generated waste rather than regulating it at the production stage. And the damage falls squarely on us in the present and on future generations.

The surest way to solve this problem is to produce and consume less plastic . To achieve this, strong distribution regulations are required both domestically and internationally, and companies must also seriously recognize the threat that food packaging materials pose to consumer health.

Greenpeace demands on government and corporations

The government must stop the use of hazardous plastics and chemicals based on the precautionary principle.
Companies must set goals and commit to achieving “zero release” of microplastics and hazardous chemicals from packaging to food.
You must stop giving consumers false reassurance regarding "microwave-safe" containers.
We must stop using single-use and plastic packaging materials and introduce a reuse system.
In a world flooded with plastic, it may feel that individual action alone has its limits. However, if we raise our voices together, we can certainly bring about change.

Polluting corporations must be held accountable for the plastic problem, and governments must implement strong policies and systemic changes to protect the health of people and the planet. Please join Greenpeace in demanding powerful policy changes so that our government and the world can end the plastic era!
