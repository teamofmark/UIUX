The reason why you can't buy trash bags because of the war in Iran
[작성자 부분]
Text: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
The war between the United States and Iran is destroying lives across the Middle East. The greatest victims of this conflict are none other than civilians. Having lost their homes, they now face another threat: an environmental catastrophe. Greenpeace urges an immediate halt to acts of force and a diplomatic solution through dialogue. Furthermore, we must confront the fundamental systemic flaws that perpetuate this tragic conflict.

[문단 - 01]
Since the outbreak of war, the prices of everyday necessities—from bottled water, baby formula, and groceries to shoes and even lipstick—have skyrocketed. This is no mere coincidence. Behind it lie oil and plastic.

[부제 - 01]
Plastic made from petroleum
    [문단 - 01]
    As the war disrupted oil supplies, gasoline prices at gas stations began to fluctuate wildly. However, this was just the beginning. The instability of the oil supply immediately affected grocery stores, pharmacies, and toy shops. Almost every item we use—from shampoo to delivery containers, disposable cups, snack bags, and plastic packaging for fruit—is made from petroleum.

    [이미지 - 01]

    [문단 - 02]
    How is plastic made? Crude oil is refined to extract naphtha, which is then broken down to obtain ethylene and propylene, from which synthetic resin—the raw material for plastic—is produced. This resin is used to make the water bottles, cosmetic containers, and fabrics we use every day. Ultimately, plastic is simply another form of petroleum.
     
    [문단 - 03]
    If the Strait of Hormuz, through which one-fifth of the world's oil and gas trade passes, is blocked, the supply chain for petrochemical raw materials worth $20 billion to $25 billion annually will be paralyzed. This is precisely why plastic resin prices have surged by more than 30% and toy raw material costs have skyrocketed by 55% in the past month.

[부제 - 02]
Naphtha, the rice of the petrochemical industry
    [문단 - 01]
    Naphtha (extracted during the crude oil refining process) is the "rice" of the petrochemical industry. In particular, Korea is a prime example of a country highly dependent on naphtha imports. With the United States, Saudi Arabia, and Russia controlling 40% of the world's oil supply, countries with high import dependency are taking a direct hit, and Asian nations, including Korea, are inevitably the most vulnerable. Last year, 70% of the naphtha bound for Asia passed through the Strait of Hormuz, a disputed region. This is why President Lee Jae-myung, having recognized this vulnerability, emphasized a transition to a "plastics-free economy" along with a call for peace.

How long are you going to live relying on plastic? 
    [문단 - 01]
    Over the past five years, we have experienced three massive supply chain shocks: COVID-19, the war in Ukraine, and the recent crisis in Iran. Each time, plastic became the Achilles' heel that shook our economy. Now, the conclusion boils down to one thing: breaking our dependence on fossil fuels is the only way to save the planet and safeguard our economic security.
    Just as renewable energy brings independence in electricity supply, reuse systems liberate food supply chains from the power of oil. This goes beyond mere environmental protection; it is about building 'national resilience' that remains unshaken when certain forces weaponize resources.

[부제 - 04]
You must decide to part ways with plastic.
    [문단 - 01]
    Plastic production will be the primary cause of future increases in oil demand. Europe is already leading the international trend with strong de-plasticization policies, and production reduction is being addressed as a major agenda item in the 'International Plastics Convention.' The government must align with these international trends and formulate domestic policies to fundamentally resolve the crisis we currently face. Greenpeace is urging the Korean government and the international community to establish comprehensive de-plasticization measures, including reductions in production stages, and to formulate a strong convention.

    [문단 - 02]
    The war in Iran is not merely a conflict taking place on the other side of the globe. The repercussions of the war have permeated the prices of our shopping baskets and garbage bags. We must decide whether to remain trapped in the shackles of this crisis or to become free. The alternatives are already prepared. All that is needed now is our determination.
