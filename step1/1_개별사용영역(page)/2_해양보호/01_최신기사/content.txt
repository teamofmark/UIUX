The Journey Towards the Launch of a Global Maritime Treaty (BBNJ) to Protect the High Seas
[문단 - 01]
Since 2008, Greenpeace has published reports on the threats facing the high seas, exposing gaps and failures in global ocean governance and campaigning for the establishment of a network of marine protected areas.
This has served as a starting point for consistently raising the international community's awareness of the need for a global marine treaty (BBNJ).
[문단 - 02]
Meanwhile, the international community, centered around the UN, has begun full-scale institutional discussions. Following the agreement to initiate negotiations at the 2015 UN General Assembly, six intergovernmental meetings were held between 2018 and 2023, leading to the historic BBNJ agreement reached in New York in March 2023. The treaty was formally adopted at the UN General Assembly in June, and on September 20, each country began the signature and ratification process, marking the beginning of the journey toward achieving 60 ratifications.

The Footsteps of Greenpeace Seoul Office
Greenpeace Seoul Office, which has been working with citizens on the journey to protect the public sphere, also leaves a deep mark.


Greenpeace Seoul Office has launched the '30x30 Campaign' in Korea and has embarked on a full-scale campaign to protect public waters together with citizens. © Greenpeace / Sungwoo Lee
2019 – 30x30, Launching a Promise to Protect the High Seas
The Seoul office launched the "30x30 Campaign" in Korea, joining citizens in a full-scale campaign to protect public waters.
→ This became the starting point for expanding the public waters protection agenda in Korea and laid the foundation for citizen participation in government.

2022 – 4.5 Million Voices Reach the Korean Government

In 2022, 4.5 million signatures from global citizens were delivered to the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries, urging the Korean government's support ahead of UN negotiations. © Sungwoo Lee / Greenpeace
We delivered a petition signed by 4.5 million global citizens to the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries, urging the Korean government's support ahead of UN negotiations.
→ We joined citizens in rallying our voices and pressured Korea to take a responsible role in ocean conservation within the international community.

August 2022 – Message from the Sky: Seoul Forest Marine Protection Drone Show

Greenpeace Seoul held a drone show at Seoul Forest Park on August 18, 2022, calling for the strong adoption of the "Blank for the Ocean Treaty (BBNJ)." 300 drones illuminated the Seoul sky with images of marine life, including whales, sea turtles, and penguins, along with the message "Protect the Ocean," highlighting the urgency of ocean protection. © Greenpeace / Sungwoo Lee
Ahead of the 5th BBNJ Intergovernmental Meeting, a marine conservation drone show was held in Seoul Forest, visually demonstrating the 30x30 goal through citizen participation.
→ This attracted media attention and public outreach, and led the Korean government to strengthen its support for the Global Marine Treaty (BBNJ) since the 5th intergovernmental meeting.

2023 – Documentary "Signal: The Last Signal from the Sea" documents the last signal from the sea.

The documentary "Signal: The Last Signal of the Ocean," produced with the support of over 3,000 citizens, spread the message of ocean conservation. © Boarderless Lab
We began production on the documentary "Signal: The Ocean's Last Signal," raising awareness of the ocean crisis and gathering support from citizens.
→ With over 3,000 citizens donating, we secured the momentum to produce a documentary that powerfully conveys the message of ocean conservation.

June 2024 – The Power of Citizens Gathered Through Puzzles

To mark World Oceans Day, Greenpeace Seoul held a drawing event with future generations of children at Seoul Olympic Park on June 1, 2024. © Greenpeace / Yeo-sun Park
On World Oceans Day, approximately 120 citizens participated in the "Puzzle Together Ocean Protection" performance.
→ This visually demonstrated citizen participation and directly pressured the Korean government to ratify the agreement.

February 2025 – Citizens' Voices Resonate in the National Assembly

On February 3, 2025, we held a press conference at the National Assembly with the office of Representative Wi Seong-gon and the Korean Marine Protection Civil Society Solidarity, urging the ratification of the Global Marine Treaty.
At the National Assembly, Representative Wi Seong-gon's office and a civil society solidarity group held a joint press conference urging the South Korean government to ratify the Global Maritime Treaty.
→ They emphasized that this is a crucial opportunity for the South Korean government to strengthen its maritime leadership in the international community.

March 2025 – Signal: The Last Signal of the Sea National Assembly Premiere, Delivering on the Promise of the National Assembly

On March 4, 2025, we held a National Assembly screening of the documentary "SEAGNAL" to raise awareness of the importance of ratifying the Global Barrier-Free Maritime Treaty (BBNJ) and to urge the National Assembly to quickly approve it.
At a special screening of the documentary "Signal: The Last Signal of the Sea" held at the National Assembly, I shared the need for ratification with lawmakers and secured their commitment to expedite it.
→ This ultimately led to the unanimous ratification of the Global Barrier-Free Sea Treaty (BBNJ) at the National Assembly that same month.

April 2025 – A Signal Resounds to the International Community from Busan

To build on the momentum of the 10th Our Ocean Conference (OOC), Greenpeace held a screening of a marine documentary at the Busan Cinema Center Outdoor Theater on April 28, 2025. Approximately 600 citizens attended, sharing their perspectives on the crisis facing the marine ecosystem and empathizing with the need to protect the oceans. The event was intended to urge global leaders to swiftly ratify the Global Treaty on the Protection of the Ocean (BBNJ). © Greenpeace / Jung-geun August
To coincide with the Our Ocean Conference in Busan, a special screening of "Signal: The Last Signal of the Sea" was held at the Busan Cinema Center's outdoor theater.
→ This film conveyed the aspirations of Korean citizens to the international community and alerted the world to the need for the swift entry into force of the treaty.

July 16, 2025 – Back to the Sea, released in theaters with 3,000 sponsors

From Jeju's haenyeo (female divers) to sea defenders in Mexico, Indonesia, and Barcelona, ​​the documentary "Signal: The Last Signal of the Sea" tells the stories of those who act as masters of the ocean, not victims. © Boarderless Lab
Thanks to the support of approximately 3,000 citizens, "Signal" has officially been released in theaters.
→ This has led to a petition campaign that raises public awareness of the ocean crisis and calls for the expansion of marine protected areas worldwide.

Greenpeace's footsteps in protecting the high seas around the world
Greenpeace International, together with citizens around the world, has been making various efforts to protect the ocean.

2008–2019 – Exposing the Threat

A vulnerable blue shark was dragged ashore by a Spanish longline fishing vessel in the middle of the South Pacific.
Greenpeace's Rainbow Warrior is raising awareness of this reality and calling for the ratification of a global ocean treaty to protect the high seas and the expansion of marine protected areas. © Paul Hilton / Greenpeace
Greenpeace published five reports addressing the threats facing the high seas, exposing gaps and failures in global ocean governance.
→ This laid the foundation for drawing the international community's attention to the need to protect the high seas.

2016 – Playing the piano on the Arctic sea ice

Italian composer Ludovico Einaudi performed "Elegy for the Arctic" on an Arctic ice sheet, calling for Arctic conservation. © Pedro Armestre / Greenpeace
Italian pianist Ludovico Einaudi performed on Arctic sea ice, raising awareness of the climate crisis and ocean conservation around the world.

2018 – Antarctic Exploration Raises the Need for Protected Areas

Actor Javier Bardem joins Greenpeace on the Arctic Sunrise, aboard the iceberg-covered vessel in Cierva Bay, Antarctica, to deliver a message of commitment to ocean conservation at the ends of the Earth. © Christian Åslund / Greenpeac
Actor Javier Bardem took part in a submarine expedition to highlight the urgency of establishing an Antarctic marine protected area.

2023 – A cry from New York, a historic agreement

In March 2023, the message “Strong Global Ocean Treaty Now!” was projected onto the exterior of the Guggenheim Museum in New York, as Greenpeace urged representatives from countries negotiating a strong treaty on the UN Ocean Treaty. © Greenpeace
In March 2023, during the fifth round of negotiations for the UN Convention on the Conservation of Marine Biodiversity, the message “Strong Global Ocean Treaty Now!” was projected onto the exterior wall of the Guggenheim Museum in New York, urging representatives from each country to conclude a strong treaty.

The voices of citizens around the world echoed through downtown New York City, along with scientists' warning that "if we fail to reach an agreement now, we will not be able to protect 30% of the ocean by 2030."
→ After marathon negotiations, governments finally agreed on a strong Global Treaty on the Ocean (BBNJ), culminating in 20 years of negotiations.

60 countries have ratified the Global Barrier-Free Sea Treaty (BBNJ), now putting into practice the protection of the high seas.

Last September, the Global Barrier-Free Sea Treaty (BBNJ), humanity's first high seas protection agreement, achieved ratification by 60 countries. Now, with the treaty entering into force and the first Ocean Conference of the Parties (COP) approaching, the journey toward the "30x30" goal continues. © Greenpeace / BRIDGET FERGUSON
Last September, the Global Treaty on the Protection of the Sea (BBNJ), the world's first high seas protection agreement, finally achieved ratification by 60 countries. This historic achievement is the result of over 20 years of international negotiations and the combined actions and support of citizens around the world.

The treaty is now set to officially enter into force in January next year, 120 days from now. Following entry into force, the first Ocean Conference of the Parties (COP) will convene, where discussions on designating marine protected areas on the high seas will begin in earnest. This should be a crucial turning point toward the "30x30" goal of protecting 30% of the high seas.

This achievement is the result of the combined efforts of Greenpeace and citizens. Your participation has changed history.

But the journey doesn't end here. Now, we must effectively implement the treaty and create a robust sanctuary to protect the high seas.
