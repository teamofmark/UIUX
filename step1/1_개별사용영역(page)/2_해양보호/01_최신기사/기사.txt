The 3rd United Nations Ocean Conference (UNOC) is being held. What are the tasks of the Korean government?

[작성자 부분]
Written by: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[색상배경 - 문단]
The 10th Our Ocean Conference (OOC) was held in Busan last April.

As the host country of the OOC, the Republic of Korea has expressed its commitment to the enforcement of global maritime treaties and the expansion of marine protected areas.

And from June 9th to 13th, the 3rd United Nations Ocean Conference (UNOC) will be held in Nice, France.

Starting with OOC, what role should the Korean government play in the UNOC and the future international arena for marine conservation?

Our Commitment as OOC Host, Promises for the Ocean
[문단 - 01]
At the 10th Our Ocean Conference (OOC) held in Busan last April, the Korean government announced three key commitments to achieve the 30x30 goal:
*30X30: Designate 30% of the high seas as marine protected areas by 2030.
    [순차목록]
    [제목 - 01]
    We will actively work towards the entry into force of the BBNJ (Global Marine Treaty) - Completion date 2025.06
    Korea, the 21st ratifying country of the BBNJ, announced its commitment to actively work towards the entry into force of the BBNJ in cooperation with countries around the world.
    [문단 - 01]
    *BBNJ: Agreement on the Conservation and Sustainable Use of Marine Biodiversity in Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea
    [제목 - 02]
    We will continue to expand domestic marine protected areas - Completion date: 2030
    [문단 - 02]
    We announced that we plan to designate 30% of the domestic marine area as protected areas by 2030 and conserve and manage them. In particular, we have solidified our goal of newly designating at least one large marine protected area of ​​1,000 km² or more every year.
    [제목-03]
    Contribute to the expansion of marine protected areas on the high seas –
    [문단-03]
    As a co-sponsor of the Weddell Sea Phase 1 and Northeast Antarctic Ocean Protected Area proposals of the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources (CCAMLR) by 2030, we plan to work for the adoption of these proposals. We also announced that we will actively participate in the task group for the designation of the Sala Gómez and Nazca Rise Marine Protected Areas within the South Pacific Regional Fisheries Management Organization (SPRFMO), and in particular, we plan to contribute to the protection of the Emperor Seamount, which is under discussion within the North Pacific Fisheries Organization (NPFC), based on scientific evidence.

[부제 - 03]
Why Promises and Execution Matter in OOC
[문단 - 01]
The most urgent and concrete action now is to actively implement the expansion of ‘marine protected areas’. However, there are still tasks that need to be supplemented for actual implementation.
[문단 - 02]
Currently, less than 2% of the world’s oceans are designated as protected areas. If this continues, the international goal of protecting 30% of the world’s oceans will not be achieved until 2107.
[이미지]
[문단 - 03]
One area in particular in urgent need of protection is the Emperor Seamounts in the northern Pacific Ocean.
[문단 - 04]
This area is so rich in biodiversity that it is called an 'oasis of life'. The Emperor Seamount, which consists of about 800 underwater mountain ranges, is an ecosystem where various marine life such as large whales, dolphins, endangered sharks, and deep-sea corals live.
[문단 - 05]
However, this area is now under serious threat from indiscriminate bottom fishing and destructive fishing.
[문단 - 06]
The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) and international marine scientists have been calling for this area to be designated as a priority conservation area since 2008, but the designation has not yet been made.
[문단 - 07]
As a party to the BBNJ Agreement, the Republic of Korea should expedite the entry into force of the treaty and, through cooperation with relevant countries, promptly expand the high seas protection zone.
[문단 - 08]
In addition, it is urgent to secure a specific budget and prepare an implementation roadmap for expanding marine protected areas. There are only four and a half years left until 2030. We need practical implementation power to fulfill the promise now.
