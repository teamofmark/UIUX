[문단 - 01]
The documentary < Signal : The Last Signal of the Sea> , which tells the story of the ocean that is rapidly being devastated without our knowledge, will be released in theaters nationwide on July 16th.
[문단 - 02]
< Signal : The Last Signal of the Sea> is a documentary in which seven people from around the world, including a Spanish marine noise researcher, an Australian underwater photographer, a female diver from Jeju Island in Korea, a Mexican fisherman, and a Greenpeace activist, testify about the ocean they have seen, heard, and felt.
[문단 - 03]
< Signal : The Last Signal of the Sea> was produced over a period of two years and one month by Greenpeace Seoul, documentary production company Borderless Lab, and underwater special effects company Mangrove. Prior to its release, over 3,000 citizens who advocate for ocean protection lent their support to the production through Greenpeace.
[문단 - 04]
"The ocean has been sending us signals since the beginning of time," the documentary begins with the voice of Spanish marine noise researcher Professor Michel André. Marine life, including whales and dolphins that communicate through sound waves for food, are threatened by human-generated noise. If these creatures, who communicate through sound waves, disappear, the ocean will be left with nothing but silence. Professor André warns, "If the ocean is quiet, it's a dead ocean."
[문단 - 05]
In the documentary, Jeju Island haenyeo Lee Yu-jeong shares her perilous experience of nearly being trapped in a net while diving, as well as her experience witnessing a dolphin whose tail was also entangled in a net. Discarded fishing gear poses a serious threat to both humans and marine life.

[문단 - 06]
The Indonesian case illustrates how sea level rise can profoundly impact an individual's life. Muhammad Rusipan, who plays an Indonesian fisherman, was originally a farmer but became a fisherman after his village was flooded. His parents' graves were submerged by sea level rise, and he prays that his wife's grave will not be submerged.
[문단 - 07]
Kim Yeon-ha, a Greenpeace Oceans Campaigner, explained, "<Signal> was planned to highlight the severity of this ocean destruction and call for the protection of the high seas." Greenpeace previously held screenings of <Signal> at the National Assembly and in Busan to urge the South Korean government to ratify the Global Barrier-Free Sea Treaty (BBNJ) and the international community to swiftly implement expansion of high seas protection zones .
[문단 - 08]
Campaigner Kim continued, "The high seas cover two-thirds of the world's oceans, but the absence of strong governance has led to serious marine destruction. With the recent rise of deep-sea mining, international cooperation to stop this has become more urgent than ever." He added, "I hope <Signal> will reach more citizens and serve as a driving force to protect the oceans from silence."
[문단 - 09]
Another compelling aspect of " Signal : The Last Signal of the Sea" is its vivid visuals of oceans around the world . Cinematographer Shin Yong-soo, who specializes in underwater photography, said, "The ocean is a space that's usually hidden from view, so it's difficult to grasp the fact that the beautiful underwater environment is rapidly disappearing and suffering. However, I hope this documentary will share the ocean's warning and sense of alarm."
