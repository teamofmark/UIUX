(January 16, 2026, 2:00 PM) Street murals commemorating the official entry into force of the BBNJ Agreement, a global maritime treaty, were unveiled in countries including the United States, Japan, Germany, and Mexico. The BBNJ Agreement <strong>will enter into force simultaneously worldwide, including in Korea,</strong>  on January 17 .

Greenpeace, an international environmental organization, announced that it planned this project to commemorate the achievements of its 20-year ocean protection campaign and to convey the message that 30% of the world's oceans must be protected by 2030. Greenpeace has been campaigning for the protection of the high seas since 2008.

As the ocean is a borderless, interconnected ecosystem, this project showcased this interconnectedness by simultaneously conducting work across five continents and 13 countries: Slovenia, Austria, the Philippines, Mexico, Mauritius, Senegal, Germany, Australia, the United Kingdom, Japan, Canada, the Netherlands, and the United States. [문단 - 04]
The BBNJ Agreement met the entry into force requirements by reaching 60 ratifications in September of last year. Ratifying countries will be subject to various legal obligations starting January 17th. Accordingly, governments must establish domestic laws and administrative systems for designating marine protected areas on the high seas, conducting environmental impact assessments, and managing genetic resources. Furthermore, ratifying countries must participate in international consultations and implementation discussions, including the upcoming Ocean Conference of the Parties (Ocean COP). The South Korean government ratified the agreement in March of last year. 

[문단 - 05]
The BBNJ Agreement is considered the most significant environmental legislation since the Paris Agreement, establishing a legal foundation for the previously neglected high seas. Protecting the high seas goes beyond simply expanding protected areas; it has crucial implications for mitigating the climate crisis, preserving biodiversity, and ensuring food security for billions of people who depend on the ocean. The high seas cover 61% of the ocean, but only 0.9% is currently fully protected (absolute conservation areas) or highly protected (advanced conservation areas). 

[문단 - 06]
“Governments that have ratified the treaty must now legally implement their actions to protect the high seas,” said Lukas Meus, oceans campaigner at Greenpeace International, commenting on the BBNJ agreement’s entry into force. “To increase the 0.9% to 30% would require establishing ocean areas larger than entire continents as protected areas, and there are only four years left until the international community’s 2030 commitment.”

Kim Yeon-ha, Greenpeace Oceans Campaigner, said, “As a ratifying country, the Korean government must also prepare to designate high seas marine protected areas, including the Emperor Seamount in the North Pacific, with strong protection levels and quickly establish a domestic implementation system.” She added, “As the first Asian country to co-host the United Nations Ocean Conference (UNOC4) in 2028, Korea has a responsibility to show trust to the international community with concrete high seas protection results, not diplomatic declarations.”  

[문단 - 09]
At the 10th Our Ocean Conference (OOC) held last year, the Korean government stated its position regarding the Emperor Seamount, stating, "We will contribute to ensuring that appropriate protection measures are established based on the best scientific information." The Emperor Seamount is home to numerous endangered and endemic species, making it a key water area that the scientific community and the international community have identified as requiring priority protection. 

[문단 - 10]
Meanwhile, at this year's UN General Assembly, 16 countries launched the Ocean Pioneers Coalition, pledging to ratify ocean treaties and support a moratorium on deep-sea mining. Greenpeace is urging governments to join this coalition.
