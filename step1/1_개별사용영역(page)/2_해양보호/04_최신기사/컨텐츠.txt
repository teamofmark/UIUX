[Statement] South Korea Confirmed as Host of UNOC4: "Specific Plans for Protecting the High Seas Must Be Presented"
[작성자 부분]
Written by: Greenpeace

[비 순차 목록 - 01]
South Korea confirmed as host of the 4th UN Ocean Conference in 2028.
[비 순차 목록 - 02]
BBNJ to enter into force in 2026, 30x30 in 2030… A timely and important ocean conference
[비 순차 목록 - 03]
Greenpeace Demands: "We must take action, not just words, to designate pollution protection zones." 
[문단 - 01]
(December 10, 2025) At the UN General Assembly held at UN Headquarters on the 9th (New York City local time), the South Korean government was officially confirmed as a co-host, along with Chile, of the Fourth United Nations Ocean Conference (UNOC4) in 2028. Hosting UNOC4 will be a significant opportunity for South Korea to demonstrate its leadership in marine conservation within the international community, and to this end, the government must proactively engage in protecting the high seas. 
[문단 - 02]
The UNOC is the world's largest multilateral conference that discusses the conservation and sustainable use of marine ecosystems, the law of the sea, and other issues. Governments, international organizations, the scientific community, and civil society participate to determine the future direction of marine protection. 
[문단 - 03]
The confirmation of the UNOC4 host country is particularly significant in its timing. First, the BBNJ Agreement, the first-ever global maritime treaty governing high seas protection, is set to officially enter into force on January 17, 2026. This will mark a turning point, as the international community moves from declaration to implementation of high seas protection. Furthermore, UNOC4 is even more significant as it is being held two years ahead of the "30x30" goal of protecting 30% of the world's oceans by 2030.
[문단 - 04]
The South Korean government has repeatedly expressed its commitment to ocean protection. In March, the government became the first East Asian country to ratify the BBNJ Agreement. It also pledged to the international community to achieve the 30x30 goal at the Kunming-Montreal Biodiversity Framework (KMGBF) in December 2022. At the 10th Our Ocean Conference (OOC) held in Busan in April, the government announced several pledges to protect the high seas and pledged to contribute to the international community.
[문단 - 05]
Specific pledges made at the time of the OOC include expanding the Southern Ocean Marine Protected Area and participating in discussions on the Salaz y Gomez–Nazca Ridge Protected Area in the South Pacific. Regarding the Emperor Seamount in the North Pacific, currently under discussion by the North Pacific Fisheries Organization (NPFC), the organization pledged to "contribute to ensuring that appropriate protection measures are established based on the best scientific information." 
[문단 - 06]
For these declarations to truly bring about change, they must be followed by concrete implementation plans. The confirmation as host of UNOC4 signifies that the time has come for Korea to take concrete action. To demonstrate responsible leadership as the host of UNOC4 and to promote the level of action demanded by the international community, the Korean government must implement the following actions. 

[이미지 - 01]

[부제 - 01]
Greenpeace demands
    [비 순차 목록 - 01]
    <strong>Officially announce a proposal for a Marine Protected Area ( MPA) in high-priority waters, including the Emperor Seamount in the North Pacific.</strong> The Emperor Seamount is home to numerous species, including endangered and endemic species, and has been identified by the scientific community and the international community as a key area in need of priority protection. However, it has long suffered from bottom-water trawling and bottom-net fishing, necessitating urgent protection. The Korean government must develop and present a clear MPA proposal plan for this area.
    [비 순차 목록 - 02]
    <strong>Domestic laws implementing the BBNJ Agreement will be enacted in line with international standards.</strong> The BBNJ Agreement, a global maritime treaty ratified by 60 countries, will enter into force on January 17th. Accordingly, implementing laws that guarantee independent scientific advice, alignment with international norms, and stakeholder participation are essential.
    [비 순차 목록 - 03]
    <strong>As the host of UNOC4, we must present a concrete roadmap for implementing the 30x30 goal in our territorial waters and high seas.</strong> This plan must include a phased schedule, criteria for selecting target areas, an international cooperation structure, and regulatory and enforcement mechanisms. For example, France (co-hosting with Costa Rica), which previously hosted UNOC3, pledged to designate the world's largest marine protected area and the largest no-take zone in French Polynesian waters. Furthermore, French President Emmanuel Macron has made it clear that the international community must ban deep-sea mining.
    [비 순차 목록 - 04]
    <strong>We will proactively advocate and exert diplomatic efforts to ensure that proposals for marine protected areas with strong protection levels that meet international standards are discussed and adopted.</strong> The key to establishing high seas protected areas is the degree of protection. Beyond mere protection zones, protection measures should be applied at the level of no-take zones recommended by the international community. Currently, only 0.9% of the world's oceans are designated as absolute or highly protected areas. 
    [비 순차 목록 - 05]
    <strong>Develop and disclose a plan for scientific research on the high seas for "conservation purposes."</strong> Long-term, systematic, conservation-focused scientific research is essential for high seas protection policies. The government's ongoing "2027 Hwangjehaesan Marine Scientific Survey" should be made public with clear conservation objectives and a plan to ensure it serves as a starting point for the conservation of high seas biodiversity and vulnerable marine ecosystems. Furthermore, the survey results should not be limited to mere research but should be linked to discussions on high seas protected areas and strengthened management systems.
    [비 순차 목록 - 06]
    <strong>Officially support the eradication and moratorium on deep-sea mining.</strong> Sweden, Portugal, and France, the hosts of the UN Ocean Conference, have officially supported a moratorium on deep-sea mining and have made clear their stance on ocean protection. As the host of UNOC4, the Korean government should also formally support a moratorium on deep-sea mining. This is an essential step for Korea to become a responsible host and a leader in global ocean conservation, and will contribute to reaffirming that the deep sea is a common asset for all humanity.

[문단 - 07]
Kim Yeon-ha, Greenpeace Oceans Campaigner, said, “Co-hosting UNOC4 is an opportunity for Korea to receive recognition from the international community for its leadership in marine protection, but it also gives Korea a responsibility to take action.” She added, “The government should take proactive steps to implement the BBNJ Agreement, such as establishing a plan to propose marine protected areas for waters that require priority protection, such as the Emperor Seamount.” 

[문단 - 08]
Greenpeace has been campaigning through its offices around the world to designate 30% of the high seas as marine protected areas (30x30) by 2030, emphasizing the importance of the BBNJ Agreement, which provides the legal foundation for this goal. Greenpeace plans to continue urging the Korean government to faithfully implement the BBNJ Agreement and campaign for the swift expansion of high seas marine protected areas.
