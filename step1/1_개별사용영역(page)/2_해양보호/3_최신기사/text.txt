[Report] Greenpeace Holds National Assembly Screening of Marine Documentary SEAGNAL and Urges for Speedy Ratification

[작성자부분]
Ratification of Global Maritime Treaty, Now the Ball is in the Court of the National Assembly
Written by: Greenpeace

[비순차목록]
Pollution, overfishing/mining, etc., which cover 2/3 of the world's oceans, are causing serious destruction

Ratification of Global Maritime Treaty, Only National Assembly Approval Procedure Remains

Greenpeace: “The National Assembly should take the lead and join us in ratifying the historic ocean agreement”

[문단 - 1]
(March 5, 2025) A National Assembly preview of a marine documentary was held to urge the Korean government to ratify the global marine treaty. Currently, the ratification of the global marine treaty is in the final stages.


[문단 - 2]
Greenpeace announced that on the afternoon of the 4th, it held a screening of the documentary SEAGNAL, which deals with the seriousness of ocean destruction, at the National Assembly together with former Minister of Environment and Democratic Party lawmaker Han Jeong-ae, Democratic Party lawmaker Kim Young-bae, secretary of the National Assembly Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee, and Greenpeace supporters and marine protection civil society groups.

[문단 - 3]
This screening was held to urge the National Assembly to ratify the Global Marine Treaty for the Protection of the High Seas (BBNJ Agreement/Convention on the Conservation of Biological Diversity in Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction).

[문단 - 4]
The high seas cover two-thirds of the world's oceans, but they are not under the sovereignty of each country, and are seriously destroyed by overexploitation of resources. 37% of all shark and ray species are endangered, and approximately 100 million sharks are commercially caught each year. The global shark population has decreased by 70% over the past 50 years. Recently, with the movement toward deep-sea mining, there has been an urgent need to establish governance to effectively protect and manage the high seas.

[문단 - 5]
Accordingly, the international community agreed on the BBNJ Agreement, a global maritime treaty, in early 2023, and the Korean government showed its will to protect the ocean by signing the agreement in October of that year, but has not yet ratified it. The ratification bill has currently passed the State Council and is only waiting for the National Assembly’s approval.

[문단 - 6]
Kim Yeon-ha, a Greenpeace ocean campaigner, said, “The BBNJ Agreement is very significant as it is the first legally binding treaty aimed at conserving marine life in the high seas,” adding, “However, it can only come into effect after being ratified by more than 60 countries, so Korea should hurry and join this movement.” As of the 5th, 18 countries have completed ratification.

[문단 - 7]
Kim campaigner continued, “The ball is now in the National Assembly’s court. Through this screening, I hope that SEAGANL, the signal for ocean protection created by Greenpeace and citizens, will reach the National Assembly,” and added, “I hope that the National Assembly will take the lead in pushing for rapid ratification so that the international agreement to expand the high seas marine protected areas can be implemented, and join us in this historic step toward ocean protection.”

[문단 - 8]
Rep. Han Jeong-ae said, “Once the global ocean treaty is ratified and comes into effect, a global network of marine protected areas will be established, securing space for marine life to recover and thrive.” She added, “In particular, since the Our Ocean Conference (OOC), a high-level international conference discussing marine pollution, climate change, and maritime security, is scheduled to be held in Busan in April, we must quickly pass the ratification bill.”

[문단 - 9]
Rep. Kim Young-bae said, “The BBNJ agreement was submitted to the National Assembly’s Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee on February 26 and is scheduled to be discussed at the committee’s bill subcommittee on the 6th.” He added, “Since the ratification bill only requires the National Assembly’s consent procedure, as the floor leader of the committee, I will do my best to ensure that it passes the National Assembly as soon as possible.”

[문단 - 10]
The documentary Signal screened that day contains the stories of witnesses who encountered the sea in crisis, with the meaning of the last signal from the sea. Appearing in the documentary are an Indonesian farmer, an Australian underwater photographer, a Korean Jeju female diver, a Mexican fisherman, and a Spanish ocean noise researcher.

[문단 - 11]
Signal was co-produced by Greenpeace, documentary production company Borderless Lab, and underwater special filming specialist Mangrove over the course of 1 year and 8 months, and was supported by over 3,000 citizens who support ocean protection. ( See synopsis for details .)

[문단 - 12]
Meanwhile, Greenpeace has played an important role from the early stages of global marine treaties, such as by creating a new treaty in accordance with the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea in 2005 to protect biodiversity and create marine protected areas on the high seas. Since then, it has been campaigning to designate marine protected areas in about 20 countries, and in Korea, since 2019, it has been campaigning to achieve the global marine conservation goal of designating at least 30% of the entire ocean as protected areas by 2030.
