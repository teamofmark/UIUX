[문단배너 - 01]
Campaign Objectives
In order to protect the ocean as a sustainable space, we are requesting UN member states, including Korea, to enact a maritime treaty and designate 'marine protected areas' with international binding force.

[문단사진구성]
Did you know?
The high seas, which cover 61% of the total ocean surface, are completely unprotected from human profit-seeking activities. Despite the most effective way to protect the oceans, marine protected areas, where no human activity, including fishing, scientific experiments, or tourism, is permitted, cover only 2% of the world’s oceans.

[문단배너 - 02]
Problem
The oceans are suffering from climate change, plastic pollution, deep-sea mining, oil drilling, fishing, and other human activities for profit.

Marine life populations, including sea turtles, coral reefs, and sharks, have declined dramatically over the past few decades. The problem is that there is no international agreement to protect the high seas, or waters beyond national jurisdiction.

Greenpeace is demanding that the international community fulfill its responsibility to protect the high seas by enacting a UN marine convention that will serve as the basis for designating 'marine protected areas'.

[주제목록구성 - 02]
Campaign activities
Greenpeace is conducting research and investigations using environmental surveillance vessels to raise awareness of the urgency of concluding a UN maritime treaty, and together with Ocean Defenders, a civic group that delivers Greenpeace's message of ocean protection, Greenpeace has demanded that the Korean government delegation conclude a UN maritime treaty.

June 2022 (Completed)
To celebrate World Oceans Day, we are holding a giveaway event for 'sea creature illustration wallpapers' including whales, penguins, and coral reefs to convey the message of expanding the high seas marine protection zone.

July 2022 (Completed)
Greenpeace Antarctica Photo Exhibition Vividly Captures the Antarctic Ecosystem as Seen from an Environmental Monitoring Ship Held in Yongsan, Seoul

August 2022 (Completed)
Using 300 drones, endangered marine species were launched into the night sky of Seoul Forest Park, urging world leaders, including Korea, to sign a global marine treaty.

February 2023 (Completed)
Marine protection screening held on the large screen at Hangang Park to urge the conclusion of a maritime treaty

March 2023 (Completed)
A historic 'global ocean treaty' to protect the world's oceans was signed at the UN Convention on the Conservation of Marine Biodiversity Emergency Meeting held in March 2023.
