[Statement] A Comprehensive Plastic-Free Plan Without Production Reductions: A Comprehensive Review!
[작성자부분]
Written by: Greenpeace

[문단 - 01]
(December 23) Today, at the "National Debate on Comprehensive Plastic-Free Measures," President Lee Jae-myung's campaign promise and a key government policy initiative, the "Comprehensive Plastic-Free Measures," were unveiled for the first time. While this comprehensive plan was expected to reflect the new administration's aspirations for plastic-free action, the revealed content lacked direction, and the planning was poor and complacent. 

[문단 - 02]
The government's announcement today was merely a list of policies focused on recycling and waste management, largely unchanged from previous measures. It also omitted provisions to normalize the previously weakened regulations on single-use products. Furthermore, the Ministry of Climate Change's opaque approach to gathering opinions and disclosing only limited information during the process of developing a comprehensive plan to phase out plastics is problematic. Today's "National Debate on the Comprehensive Plan to Phase Out Plastics" was merely a formality, lacking public participation or discussion.

[이미지 - 01]

[문단 - 03]
The comprehensive plan we need now to phase out plastics is one that considers the current level of plastic pollution, the current crisis, and international trends, and aims to address the fundamental problem. While the macro goal of reducing mining in this comprehensive plan is significant, it only includes goals and plans for recycling (recycled plastic), a solution at the waste level, rather than goals and implementation targets at the production level. Without specific targets and annual implementation plans for reducing total plastic production at the raw material level, the plan to phase out plastics remains mere slogans. 

[문단 - 04]
The comprehensive plan to phase out plastics has announced its final draft will be finalized early next year. The Ministry of Climate Change must lead the transformation of industry and society through production reduction targets, which are at the core of the "plastic phase-out." Civil society groups call for a comprehensive review of the comprehensive plan to phase out plastics and once again strongly urge the establishment of specific targets for reducing plastic production and annual implementation plans.
