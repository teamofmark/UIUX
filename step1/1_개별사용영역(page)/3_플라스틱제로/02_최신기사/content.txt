Another unfinished international plastics agreement: What happened in Geneva?
[작성자부분]
Written by: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
Last month, the second INC5 meeting (INC5.2) was held in Geneva, Switzerland. Despite over ten days of discussions, governments failed to reach an agreement on how to address the plastic pollution crisis. Negotiations were adjourned without a clear path forward, but one thing became clear from this meeting: we must secure a strong agreement that prioritizes the health of people and the planet over the profits of polluting corporations.

If we don't reduce production, we can't reduce pollution.

[문단 - 01]
The root cause of the plastic problem is clear: we produce too much. To protect biodiversity, keep global warming below 1.5°C, and safeguard our health, rights, and communities, we must drastically reduce global plastic production.

Studies show that if current trends continue, plastic production will triple by 2050.

However, the planet and the climate cannot handle any more plastic.

Ahead of the INC5.2 meeting, over 90 countries around the world signed the
"
[버튼 - https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/documents/The%20Nice%20wake%20up%20call%20for%20an%20ambitious%20plastics%20treaty.pdf]

also known as the "Nice Declaration," reaffirming their commitment to a strong plastics agreement.The declaration outlines key elements for a strong agreement, including: setting targets for reducing plastic production and consumption; regulating hazardous products and chemicals; expanding reuse systems; ensuring fair financing; and holding a vote at a future Conference of the Parties to break the deadlock in negotiations. 

[문단 - 02]
Last August, representatives from approximately 180 UN member states gathered in Geneva, Switzerland, for the fifth reconvened meeting of the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee (INC5.2) to negotiate a global plastics agreement. The meeting, an extension of the INC5 meeting held in Busan last year, saw a majority of countries express support for a robust agreement, including production reductions. However, the negotiations stalled again during the process of coordinating details and finding a common solution. This was because some countries, seeking to maintain the current era of plastic overload, exploited the unanimity system to drive the negotiations into a deadlock. Ultimately, the chair presented two drafts, neither of which met the agreement's objectives. The discussions stalled again, and we returned home uncertain of when, where, or how the next round of discussions would unfold.

[문단 - 03]
The plastic pollution crisis is also a public health crisis. However, reaching an agreement has been delayed for two years, and governments remain slow to act. Even more problematic is the fact that some countries are exploiting the unanimity requirement to obstruct the establishment of strong targets, thereby pushing us back into a system of fossil fuel dependence, pollution, and destruction.

3. Silent Protest by International Civil Society Solidarity
On August 9th, during the mid-term review meeting of the INC5.2 conference, international civil society solidarity groups, including Greenpeace, gathered at the entrance to the conference hall and staged a silent protest, criticizing the stalled meeting and the process that was undermining two years of discussion. It's Time for the Plastics Industry to Exit
Like plastic pollution, the fossil fuel and petrochemical industries are omnipresent in our lives. They appear at local events, intrude into eco-friendly conferences, sponsor public events and sporting events, and spread greenwashing messages through media advertisements. The International Plastics Convention is no exception. Every INC meeting is swarmed with industry lobbyists. According to
[버튼 - https://www.ciel.org/news/inc-5-2-lobbyist-analysis/]
a report by the Center for International Environmental Law (CIEL) , a whopping 234 lobbyists representing the fossil fuel and petrochemical industries registered for this year's INC5.2 conference, the largest attendance ever.

[문단 - 02]
Before the meeting began, Greenpeace UK released a shocking report revealing that the world's largest petrochemical companies have been systematically lobbying to prevent plastic production cuts while simultaneously expanding their plastics businesses and reaping huge profits. According to the report, since negotiations for the international plastics agreement began in November 2022, just seven companies have produced 1.4 million tonnes of plastic—enough to fill 5.5 waste trucks per minute. These companies, backed by major industry groups, have sent 70 lobbyists to the negotiating table to obstruct discussions.

Greenpeace International and the Centre for International Environmental Law (CIEL) have sent

a letter

to the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the INC Secretariat , formally requesting that lobbyists be excluded from all future discussions on the plastics agreement.

Is multilateral cooperation still valid?

[문단 - 01]
As negotiations for the International Plastics Convention continue, the limitations and crises of international cooperation are becoming increasingly apparent. With complex conflicts and vested interests intertwined among nations, solutions for global environmental and social justice remain a distant prospect. Global problems require global solutions, and fundamental solutions are impossible without international rules and agreements. It's time for a change. During the INC5.2 meeting, Greenpeace representatives repeatedly shared the meme "The walls of politics are brutally high" in the group chatroom. In this harsh reality, the more we use this meme, the more we are reminded that the voices and solidarity of citizens who desire a plastic-free future are the greatest force. Citizen participation and solidarity are powerful weapons that can mobilize leaders around the world.

After Geneva, where do we go?

[문단 - 01]
We cannot and will not stop. Our future requires a new and powerful international agreement on plastics that: ▲ reduces plastic production; ▲ protects the health and rights of our communities, including vulnerable populations; ▲ protects biodiversity; and ▲ mitigates the climate crisis. The failure of Geneva to reach an agreement must not be repeated in the upcoming conferences. Conflicts with the interests of the fossil fuel industry are inevitable in the process of ending plastic pollution. We cannot expect effective solutions while ignoring the core of the problem. There is no time for hesitation. We must have the courage and perseverance to forge a strong agreement. The health of ourselves and future generations, as well as the future of our communities and the planet's ecosystem, depend on it.
