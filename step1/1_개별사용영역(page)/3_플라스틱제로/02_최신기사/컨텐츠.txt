5 Ways to Spend the Holidays Plastic-Free
[작성자부분]
Written by Sarah King / Greenpeace Plastics Campaigner

[문단 - 배경강조]
With the end of the year approaching, why not prepare a special gift for the planet instead of the plastic we carelessly use and throw away? We'll introduce five ways to practice a "plastic-free year-end" that will help you overcome the temptation of consumption and create a happy, plastic-free year. Join Greenpeace now and join the global wave of change to stop plastic pollution!

[문단 - 01]
It's a season for spending precious time with family and friends, sharing delicious food, and ending the year in our own way. However, as the end of the year approaches, our spending also increases. We often feel compelled to constantly buy new things to make the year-end more enjoyable and special.
[문단 - 02]
But there are ways to enjoy the holidays while reducing both consumption and the inconvenient plastic waste that comes with it . With corporations globally producing 430 million tons of plastic annually, choosing a plastic-free year-end would be a wonderful Christmas gift for the planet. 

[부제 - 01]
A Christmas gift for the planet is a global reduction in plastic waste.
    [이미지 - 01]
    [문단 - 01]
    Plastic packaging from the items we buy is the most common waste generated at the end of the year. This is especially true at the end of the year, making it the most obvious and significant source of waste. And that's not all. Numerous decorative items and gifts we exchange contain a vast amount of plastic. From production to disposal, this overabundant plastic pollutes the air, water, nature, and our bodies. Furthermore, as plastics break down into microscopic pieces, they harm our health. The more plastic companies produce, the more vulnerable we become to the temptation of consumption, and the more polluted the planet becomes. For a safer and cleaner future, why not reduce plastic consumption this year and give the planet a special Christmas gift?
    [문단 - 02]
    <strong>Here are five ways to practice a "plastic-free year-end" that will reduce consumer stress and increase the planet's happiness.</strong>

 
[순차 목록 -01- 부제]
1. Create a gift culture that is not necessarily about something new, but about something new to me.
    [문단 - 01]
    Embrace vintage and retro. From thrift stores and antique shops to barter shops, flea markets, and countless online platforms, a little searching can uncover more stylish and classy vintage finds than you might expect. You'll feel the satisfaction of not buying items wrapped in plastic, and you'll be doing your part to be kinder to the planet.
    [문단 - 02]
    According to data released on Earth Overshoot Day, by July 24, 2025, humanity was using resources approximately 1.8 times faster than the Earth's ability to regenerate its ecosystems. Therefore, it's time to reduce our consumption for the sake of nature. Every small act of turning a blind eye to plastic can send a powerful message: "No more plastic!" This will force major corporations to find alternatives and demonstrate to governments that we demand real solutions to the plastic problem.

[순차 목록 -02- 부제]
2. What kind of courage will you show? Show me your own courage!
    [문단 - 01]
    With the end of the year packed with appointments, why not consider bringing your own containers instead of disposable plastic when going out to gatherings? Plastic-coated paper cups, packaging materials called "bioplastics," and even reusable takeout containers aren't entirely free from microplastics and chemicals.
    [이미지 - 02]
    [문단 - 02]
    Also, keep an eye out for plastics lurking throughout your home. Large plastic spatulas and spoons, plastic utensils, cutting boards, storage containers, drinking straws, electric kettles, blenders, and coated pans all contain or are made of plastic. Studies have shown that using these plastic utensils can increase the risk of microplastic ingestion and exposure to potentially toxic chemicals. While you may not be able to change everything immediately, make small changes like switching to stainless steel or cast iron when the time comes. When hosting guests, using personalized tableware instead of disposable items or having them bring their own will make the holiday dinner even more special.

[순차 목록 -03- 부제]
3. Instead of flashy decorations, let your inner self shine.
    [문단 - 01]
    It's easy to get caught up in the glittering holiday decorations and clothes, but most glittery things are often a combination of plastic and metal. Glitter can be found in craft supplies, wrapping paper, decorations, clothing, cosmetics, children's toys, costumes, party hats, and even snow globes.
    [이미지 - 03]
    [문단 - 02]
    Furthermore, plastic glitter and sequins can easily shed into the environment and drain when washing clothes or cleaning crafts. When combined with paper or other materials, these products are less recyclable, leading most to landfills or incinerators. While some may claim to be biodegradable, their potential pollution risks make them better for the planet.
    [문단 - 03]
    Rather than focusing on flashy decorations, how about focusing on the values ​​that fill your inner self?

[순차 목록 -04- 부제]
4. Resist the urge to buy new things.
    [문단 - 01]
    Are you shopping to relieve stress or to avoid being out of style?
    [문단 - 02]
    However, discarding perfectly good items to keep up with trends places a significant burden on the planet. True minimalism doesn't come from throwing away valuable items to buy new ones, but from the habit of using them for a long time. Before purchasing something new, think twice and ask yourself if it's truly necessary. The satisfaction you derive from doing so will last much longer.

[순차 목록 -05- 부제]
5. Spend the year-end by joining the movement to change the world.
    [문단 - 01]
    Trying to eliminate plastic on your own can be exhausting. No matter how hard you try, it can feel like nothing's changing. There's a limit to how much we can change our broken plastic-addicted system on our own.

Greenpeace calls on governments worldwide to reach a strong international plastics agreement that reduces plastic production and consumption, ends our reliance on problematic plastics and chemicals, and accelerates a just transition to a reusable, "zero waste" future.
