Plc Survey 2025: Classrooms Swallowed by Plastic
137 students demand a reduction in plastic production.
[작성자부분]
Written by Kim Nam-young, Greenpeace Citizen Participation Campaigner

[문단 - 배경강조]
137 elementary, middle, and high school students across the country discovered 3,025 pieces of plastic waste in their classrooms in just one week. The 2025 PLCOK Survey, conducted in collaboration with Greenpeace and the Climate Action Teachers Group, goes beyond simple environmental surveys. Students directly write letters to companies demanding a "plastic-free tomorrow." Here are the key findings from the 2025 PLCOK Survey.

    [문단 - 03]
    The "Pick Up Plastic" campaign, abbreviated as "Plukok Survey," began in 2020. The first was a stay-at-home survey, where citizens analyzed their plastic waste for a week and entered data directly. Based on this data, Greenpeace has uncovered which companies produce the most single-use plastic, and has worked with approximately 10,000 citizens to date. This empowerment has allowed Greenpeace to demand companies be transparent about their plastic usage and reduce it.
    [문단 - 04]
    In this 2025 Plastic Waste Survey, Greenpeace took on a new challenge: working with elementary, middle, and high school students to determine how much plastic waste exists in classrooms. The survey, conducted by the Climate Action Teachers' Group and elementary, middle, and high school students, involved teachers from nine classes and clubs across the country, along with a total of 137 students.
    [문단 - 05]
    In just one week, students encountered a total of 3,025 pieces of plastic waste. The number is likely even higher if you include difficult-to-sort plastics. Over 3,000 pieces of plastic waste in a single week—what do you think? Students are already living and studying in homes and classrooms consumed by plastic.

A Concern That Started in the Classroom: A Collaboration Between Greenpeace and Teachers for Climate Action
    [문단 - 01]
    When we shared this survey idea, the teachers' biggest concern was how to easily introduce the plastic issue in the classroom. For example, during the planning process, we discovered that elementary school students were unfamiliar with the classification system or English notation for plastic, beyond the word "plastic." Therefore, it was crucial to lower the level of the materials shared to students' level so they could easily understand the story about plastic and even conduct their own research. Consequently, for this survey, we created a survey and guidebook that allowed students to add drawings and photos to the survey and engage in fun activities like "spot the same picture."
    [문단 - 02]
    <i>The most common plastic is used to hold food, drinks, and water.</i>
    [문단 - 03]
    Greenpeace pondered how to enable students to experience the research results "directly." Instead of rigid tables and text, they decided to use colored pencils and pencils. Under the guidance of a teacher from the Climate Action Teachers' Group, the students, organized into groups, examined an unfamiliar plastic classification system chart and began drawing circular graphs with colored pencils.

Meeting for Sustainable Environmental Education: 2025 Plcok Survey Workshop
    [문단 - 01]
    After data analysis began, Greenpeace and teachers gathered again at the Greenpeace Seoul office. Starting with the P-Cock survey, we discussed what else was needed to transform classrooms.
    [이미지 - 04]
    [이미지 - 04 설명]
    Greenpeace and Climate Teachers Citizens' Action met again after the July 26th poll.
    [문단 - 02]
    "How can we easily communicate the topic of plastics to students in the classroom?" was a constant concern for Greenpeace and the teachers. To ensure that discussions about plastic issues in the classroom were not just one-time activities but long-term, more was needed. After extensive discussion, we decided to extend the overall duration of the survey to semester-long observations. We also created materials that would allow students to easily discuss plastics in class.

137 Students' Voices: A "Plastic-Free Tomorrow" Letter Campaign to Businesses
    [문단 - 01]
    Students who participated in the survey decided to tell their stories and ask for their voices to change the world.
    [문단 - 02]
    Greenpeace and students created a "plastic-free tomorrow" through letters. They wrote four-line poems using words like "plastic" and "reduced production," drew scenes of people drinking water without plastic, and wrote direct demands to companies. These letters will be sent to the companies that produce plastic, becoming a voice that changes the world.
    [문단 - 03]
    The Greenpeace survey confirmed that children are already aware of the plastic problem and, moreover, want to take action to address it. Greenpeace will continue to work with citizens to explore and create changes that are both feasible in everyday life and necessary for society. More detailed survey results can be found in the report.
