[Report] How Much Microplastic Is Contained at Jeju's Gimnyeong Beach?
[강조 - 색상]
The Korean Federation of Environmental Movements (PPRM) held a forum titled "A New Beginning: Reducing Plastic Production" to mark Environment Day.
[작성자부분]
Written by: Greenpeace

[문단 - 01]
On June 4th, in celebration of Environment Day, the Plastics Solidarity held a forum to unveil the text of the international plastics agreement proposed by civil society.

[내 비순차 목록 - 01]
◌ On June 4th, the forum "A New Beginning, Reducing Plastic Production" was held by the Plastic Roots Alliance (a solidarity working to eradicate the plastic problem), comprised of 16 domestic and international civic groups. The forum, themed "Beat Plastic Pollution," marked World Environment Day in 2025 and announced for the first time "The Ambitious International Plastics Agreement We Want." The forum presentation materials  can be found at the following link ( https://readmore.do/fZba ).

[내 비순차 목록 - 02]
◌ Lee Jong-soo, team leader of the Ocean Monitoring Research Team at the East Asia Sea Community, who gave the keynote speech, presented the results of a microplastic survey conducted in collaboration with Greenpeace on Gimnyeong Beach in Jeju. The survey found that the concentrations of large and medium-sized microplastics were approximately four and 21 times higher than the national average, respectively. The primary source of contamination was identified as expanded polystyrene (EPS) derived from aquaculture buoys. Furthermore, the concentration of pellets was more than 10 times higher than the national beach average. Team leader Lee warned, “This survey demonstrates how widespread and complex plastic pollution is, transcending the management of specific beaches. It is a problem that requires urgent, fundamental solutions. Plastic waste is generated throughout the entire process, from production and use to distribution and disposal, and ultimately flows into the ocean. Therefore, the debris accumulating on beaches is merely the surface of marine pollution, but rather a symbolic result that reveals the complex structural problems that lie behind it.”

[내 비순차 목록 - 03]
◌ Next, Byun Su-bin, the representative of the marine environmental group Deepda Jeju, gave a presentation based on data collected through freediving on marine debris. According to data on hazardous marine debris collected from January to May of this year, 20,532 kg of marine debris included 54 syringes, 260 medicine bottles, and 155 other pharmaceutical items. Byun Su-bin emphasized, “Among marine debris, plastic medical waste is particularly dangerous in terms of its toxicity and potential for dispersal, and requires meticulous and thorough management. However, it is currently not properly managed, and because it moves across borders with ocean currents, a single country’s response is limited. Therefore, an international joint monitoring system is essential, and the only solution that can implement it is an ambitious international plastics agreement.”

◌ Next, Director Kim Won of the Occupational Health and Safety Research Institute revealed the results of a citizen-led biomonitoring project that “Koreans are contaminated with various environmental hormones due to hazardous chemicals used in plastics, and their exposure levels are higher than in other countries.” He emphasized that among the chemicals that we are unintentionally exposed to in an environment surrounded by plastic, environmental hormones that have been proven to be harmful can cause various diseases such as cancer, developmental and reproductive toxicity, precocious puberty, obesity, and diabetes. Representative hazardous substances in plastics include phthalates, perfluorinated compounds (PFAS), and organophosphate flame retardants, and if we are not sufficiently protected from them, the risk of serious diseases such as precocious puberty, ADHD, breast cancer, and infertility increases. Director Kim pointed out that “Plastics pollute the Earth, and hazardous substances in plastics threaten the environment and human health,” and added, “Our country cannot escape their impact. This is why we need global regulations on plastic production and strict regulations on hazardous substances in plastics.”

[내 비순차 목록 - 05]
◌ Arpita Bhagat, Asia Pacific Plastics Policy Officer for the Global Alliance for Incinerator Alternatives (GAIA), which has been actively involved in negotiations for the international plastics agreement, pointed out that “the current agreement, which has failed to reach a final agreement and is at a standstill, is weakly binding and lacks chemical regulations, financial support, and practical implementation measures.” She predicted that “substantial progress will likely be made between negotiation sessions, with discussions likely to focus on future schedules and the election of a chair. Furthermore, an ambitious agreement requires strong policies, financial support, and leadership from countries like Korea.”

[내 비순차 목록 - 06]
◌ Lastly, Yoo Hye-in, head of the Resource Circulation Team at the Korean Federation for Environmental Movements, and Park Jeong-eum, head of the Resource Circulation Team at the Seoul Federation for Environmental Movements, announced the "Ambitious International Plastics Agreement We Want," which centers on the reduction of primary plastic polymers and Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR) among the parties. This agreement, based on the "Chair's Text," the official foundational document for the 5th Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee negotiations released on December 1, 2024, focuses on the entire plastics life cycle, and excludes any sentences that weaken the agreement's binding force. In particular, it clearly reflects the principle that "those responsible pay for the costs, and those most affected receive support first." The two activists, referring to the new government that was launched that day, emphasized, “In order for President-elect Lee Jae-myung to implement the plastic phase-out policy he promised as a campaign pledge, he must agree to an agreement document that includes a reduction in plastic production at the 5.2nd Intergovernmental Negotiation Committee, and he must play a leading role in the international community to conclude an ambitious agreement with the participation of ministers in the Korean delegation.”
