Warning from Jeju: The golden time to solve the plastic problem is now

[작성자 부분]
Written by: Greenpeace

[색상배경 - 문단]
Microplastics that flow into the ocean accumulate in living organisms and eventually penetrate human tables and bodies. According to the Jeju beach survey, the pollution level is several times higher than the national average, and the main culprits are expanded polystyrene (EPS) and industrial waste pellets. The fundamental solution to this pollution is not recycling, but ‘reducing production’ of plastics, which is why the new government must step forward to adopt a strong international plastics agreement that includes reducing production.

'Micro (nano)plastics' smaller than 5mm are found in seawater, beach sand, and even in our bodies.
Plastics are essential in modern society, but the damage caused by indiscriminate plastic production poses a serious threat to the global environment and eventually comes back to us. In particular, 'micro (nano) plastics' less than 5 mm in size are found in seawater, beach sand, and even in our bodies. The plastics we throw away are broken down by light and wind into microplastics, and various organisms ingest the microplastics. And these organisms are consumed as human food materials and accumulate in our bodies. Research on the effects of microplastics accumulating in our bodies through various routes is pouring out every day.
Greenpeace investigated the status of microplastic pollution at Gimnyeong Beach in Jeju in conjunction with the East Asia Sea Community Ocean and World Environment Day held in Jeju. This investigation was the first to not only determine the number of microplastics on Jeju Beach but also analyze their components, and the microplastic pollution on Jeju Beach was more shocking than expected.

Facing the reality of microplastics piled up on Jeju beaches

[설명]
Greenpeace, together with the East Asia Marine Community Oceans, investigated the state of microplastic pollution at Gimnyeong Beach in Jeju Island to mark World Environment Day.
[문단 - 01]
The average density of large microplastics at Gimnyeong-ri Beach was 954/m², which was about 3.8 times higher than the national average of 20 sites in 2016. Expanded polystyrene (EPS), which is generated by the fragmentation of Styrofoam buoys, was identified as the main source of pollution, accounting for 84.1% of the total. In addition, EPS is mostly white, so 87% of the investigated particles were white, and most of them were accumulated on the drifting line and the rear along the waves and wind.
[문단 - 02]
Medium-sized plastics (5-25mm) were also not easy. The average density was 289/m², and 73.7% of them were EPS, which is on the verge of decomposing into microplastics, increasing the risk to the marine ecosystem. In addition, plastic raw material ‘pellets’, which appear to have leaked from the industry, were found at more than 10 times the average of 20 national sites in 2016, clearly revealing the lack of management in the related industry.

Korean government consistently silent on plastic problem
[문단 - 01]
The theme of World Environment Day 2025, co-hosted and organized by the Korean government and UNEP, has also been selected as 'End Plastic Pollution'. The Korean government is receiving attention from the international community not only for World Environment Day 2025, but also for hosting the 5th Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee (INC5), which was announced as the final negotiation for the International Plastics Agreement last year. However, contrary to expectations, the Korean government has been consistently quiet and has not exercised its influence. The Lee Jae-myung government must be different. The new government must make choices for the future, not represent the momentary interests of some companies.

Many people think of the plastic problem as something that happens 'after it's thrown away'. However, plastic recycling is a solution to the waste disposal stage, and the global recycling rate is only about 9%, the number of recycling times is very limited, and the more it is recycled, the more toxic substances are produced. In the future, the use of plastic itself should be reduced, and companies should establish reuse and refill systems so that consumers who do not want to use plastic can choose. Reducing plastic production and consumption is the only way to solve the plastic pollution problem for sure and the end.
Our planet cannot afford to produce any more plastic.
