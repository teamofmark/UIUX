Global Plastics Agreement: A New Year, Negotiations Continue

[작성자 부분]
Written by: Greenpeace

[문단 - 배경강조]
At the end of 2024, government representatives gathered in Busan for the fifth meeting for the global plastics agreement. Greenpeace stood up to the sabotage of the petrochemical industry and oil producing countries for a 'strong global plastics agreement'. Here are five key events that took place during the meeting.

[문단 - 1]
In December 2024, the fifth meeting (INC5) for the International Plastics Convention in Busan, Korea, ended. This meeting, which was supposed to be the last, ended up being the last. In other words, the UN member states failed to finalize the text of the International Plastics Convention at this meeting. We know that we urgently need a strong international plastics convention to protect our own health, biodiversity, and climate. Here is a summary of the main points and events that occurred at this meeting, including why the convention was not concluded.


[순차목록]
1. Signature delivery: Citizens directly deliver the voices of global citizens.


[문단 - 2]
Greenpeace, along with renowned poet Nikita Gill, WWF and Break Free From Plastic, delivered a petition with nearly 3 million signatures to US Senator Jeff Merkley and Juliet Kabera, executive director of Rwanda's Environmental Management Agency.

[문단 - 3]
The petition, which has been gathering attention for the past two years, was delivered with a globe symbolizing the voices of millions of people around the world demanding a reduction in plastic production.

[문단 - 4]
Greenpeace also conveyed the voices of citizens in various ways, along with signatures.
Together with artist Dan Acher, they created a giant snow flag with portraits of citizens from all over the world and flew it over Busan .
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/press/32485/presslease-wearewatching2024/)
They also marched with over a thousand domestic and international citizens to demand a strong agreement .
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/press/32450/presslease-massmobilization2024/)

[문단 - 5]
Through this, Greenpeace conveyed the voice of the world's citizens to government representatives and sent a strong message that we are watching their decisions. #WeAreWatching!

2. Fossil fuel industry interference: Active sabotage to neutralize everything.

[문단 - 6]
Since the first round of negotiations for the International Plastics Convention (INC1), oil and petrochemical industries have been doing everything they can to delay the talks, wasting everyone’s precious time. This meeting was no exception. 
220 big lobbyists from the fossil fuel and chemical industries attended this meeting .
(https://www.ciel.org/news/inc-5-lobbyist-analysis/)
This is the largest number of lobbyists in the five meetings so far, and far more than the 196 who attended the last four meetings. This is three times more than the Scientists’ Coalition for an Effective Plastics Convention and nine times more than the Indigenous Peoples’ Coalition.
They have been obstructing and exerting enormous influence in every meeting, including this one, to block a strong agreement.

[문단 - 7]
Greenpeace and solidarity groups have exposed their influence and disruption at conferences and called on UN member states to move forward unwaveringly to secure a strong agreement.



3. Positive change: More than 100 countries join in demanding a strong agreement that includes production cuts

[문단 - 8]
Of course, there were also positive changes. As a result of the powerful work of Greenpeace and allied groups, more than 100 UN member states representing billions of people have joined in a declaration supporting a strong agreement, including a target to reduce plastic production. These countries literally stood up for change.
Juan Carlos Monterrey, the head of the government of Panama and a prominent figure at the conference, wore his signature hat and told the truth .
(https://x.com/ciel_tweets/status/1863087489055125840)
It was a very important and meaningful moment.

[문단 - 9]
This is a clear commitment and drive from over 100 countries to a strong agreement to drastically reduce plastic production. These countries have also called on other countries to join them. This positive change, which was first seen during the two-year-long negotiation period for the agreement, must be continued in subsequent discussions.

4. Opaque meeting methods: Limiting civil society participation

[문단 - 10]
Many of the most important meetings in these negotiations were held behind closed doors, with observers such as Greenpeace not allowed to attend. This meant that civil society, indigenous groups, scientists and others were unable to see much of the negotiation process.

[문단 - 11]
This kind of opaque way of conducting meetings reduces the accountability of member states for their words and actions, and also robs government delegations who need civil society voices from having the opportunity to receive help. This is completely unfair and outrageous, and Greenpeace will fight to make sure this never happens again.


5. Result: Blocked the ‘weak agreement’

[문단 - 12]
While the global plastics agreement is not yet complete, it does mean that we have once again blocked the creation of a weak agreement. Some countries and fossil fuel industry supporters are consistently pushing for what they call a “weak plastics agreement,” and this was clearly demonstrated in the plastics agreement conference room.

[문단 - 13]
Despite vigorous efforts by oil producing countries and the petrochemical industry, Greenpeace and its partners at the conference managed to prevent a weak agreement that would do nothing to address the pollution crisis.
