Indiscriminate plastic production is not just a waste problem, but it also affects our bodies, in addition to the climate crisis, pollution of the entire environment, and loss of biodiversity.
Plastic, which is deeply embedded in our daily lives, was once a symbol of convenience, but now it is emerging as a serious problem that threatens the earth and the future. As of 2019, global plastic production has doubled from 20 years ago, reaching 460 million tons per year.
Moreover, if this increase continues, it is expected to reach about 3 times the current amount by 2050
(https://www.ciel.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Reducing-Plastic-Production-to-Achieve-Climate-Goals_Sept21_V5.pdf)
, and the result will be an acceleration of the climate crisis, destruction of the ecosystem, and an even greater impact on our bodies.

[문단 - 2]
Korea's responsibility for the plastic problem is by no means light. According to a report by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM),
as of 2016, Korea ranked third in the world in per capita plastic waste discharge, discharging 88 kg of plastic waste per person per year.
(https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/other/dels/plastics-in-the-ocean/)
In addition, according to a study by Greenpeace and Professor Jang Yong-cheol's team (Environmental Engineering Department) at Chungnam National University, plastic waste has been steadily increasing, and if we continue to use and discard it as we do now, the amount of plastic waste generated in household waste in 2030 is expected to be approximately 6,475,000 tons/year, which is 3.6 times more than in 2010. Although we are diligently separating and disposing of waste in our daily lives, the recycling rate is still far from sufficient to handle the explosive increase in plastic waste every year. (For more information,
click 'Plastic Waste Swamp, Finding Out About Plastic Consumption in Korea' .
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/update/25806/report-disposable-korea-ver2-results/)
)

[문단 - 3]
Korea is a major player not only in plastic consumption but also in plastic production. Of the top 10 plastic producers in East Asia, 70% are based in Korea. Industrial cities such as Ulsan and Yeosu produce about 20 million tons of plastic annually, which has a significant impact on the global plastic problem.

The beginning of an endless chain of pollution, the problem of oversupply

Ethylene, the raw material for polyethylene (PE), is produced in greater quantities than can be sold.
Global plastic production is overcapacitated. According to S&P data,
the amount of ethylene that can be produced worldwide in 2023 will reach 223.82 megatons, but only 176.53 megatons will actually be available for sale.
(https://www.ft.com/content/6b3f4405-a994-4fb1-b667-1f49c5357db8)
Ethylene is a hydrocarbon extracted from oil and natural gas and is the raw material for polyethylene (PE). Polyethylene is the most widely used plastic in our daily lives, used in everything from PET bottles to packaging vinyl and toys.

[문단 - 5]
In this way, the oversupply of plastic leads to greenhouse gas emissions, which is directly linked to the climate crisis. The severity of this is revealed in that 85% of greenhouse gas emissions caused by plastic come from the production process, and about 99% of plastics are produced from fossil fuels. In other words, excessive plastic production is one of the main causes of accelerating climate change.

Korea, one of the centers of plastic production

[이미지설명]

[문단 - 6]
Plastics production capacity in Korea, Japan and Taiwan is one of the major factors causing oversupply issues in the global petrochemical market.
Korea is considered a representative plastics producing country in East Asia. According to the 'Petrochemical Industry Plastic Oversupply Investigation' published by Greenpeace, the plastics production capacity of Korea, Japan, and Taiwan is one of the major factors causing the oversupply problem in the global petrochemical market. Korea's plastic production volume is approximately 19.92 million metric tons per year, followed by Japan and Taiwan with 10.34 million metric tons and 9.02 million metric tons, respectively. These three countries account for approximately 11% of the world's annual plastics production capacity.

[문단 - 7]
In addition, carbon dioxide emissions from plastic production in Korea amount to 49.55 million metric tons (CO₂e), which is comparable to the combined emissions of Japan and Taiwan. Such large-scale production inevitably leads to plastic use and plastic pollution problems, and the damage is coming back to us.

[부제목]
A vicious cycle of oversupply and excessive consumption

[이미지설명]
Greenpeace activists hold a banner demanding a reduction in plastic production in Ottawa, Canada.

[문단 - 8]
Greenpeace activists hold a banner demanding a reduction in plastic production in Ottawa, Canada.
Plastic oversupply leads to excessive plastic consumption and plastic waste problems. From 1950 to 2015, approximately 6.3 billion tons of plastics were discarded, and 79% of them are estimated to have ended up in landfills or the environment. If this trend continues, it is expected that by 2050, a whopping 12 billion tons of plastic waste will end up in landfills or in nature.

[문단 - 9]
In order to solve the plastic pollution problem, we must reduce production, which is the beginning of a vicious cycle. Korea must also take responsibility for plastic production and make efforts to reduce it, and it plays an important role in solving the global plastic pollution problem.

[부제목]
What is Korea's role in the international plastics convention being held in Busan?

[이미지설명]
Greenpeace Seoul office activists call for a strong global plastics agreement

[문단 - 10]
Greenpeace Seoul office activists call for a strong global plastics agreement
The International Plastics Convention is a critical opportunity for the world to set strong rules to reduce plastic pollution. After four rounds of negotiations since November 2022, the upcoming fifth meeting (INC5) is scheduled to take place in Busan, South Korea, from November 25. As the host country, Korea has the opportunity to play a responsible and leading role in solving the plastic pollution problem at this conference.

[문단 - 11]
Greenpeace is calling on the Korean government to take the lead in reaching an agreement that includes a strong reduction in production, which should be at least 75% worldwide by 2040. In addition, petrochemical companies should make a just transition that does not sacrifice labor rights and jobs or pass on damage to workers or the socially vulnerable to achieve carbon neutrality by 2050. This is also a way for Korea, as a plastics producer, to take responsibility and contribute to solving the climate crisis and plastics problem.

[이미지설명]
On November 19, the PPPP held a press conference titled “Korean Government Raises Issue of Plastic Oversupply and Urges Support for Strong International Plastics Agreement,” in which the PPPP pointed out the issue of plastic oversupply in the petrochemical industry and urged the Korean government to take the lead in drafting a binding international plastics agreement that includes a target to reduce plastic production.

[문단 - 12]
Korea should support legally binding production reduction targets and a carbon neutral roadmap at the 5th meeting of the Global Plastics Convention, which will be held at BEXCO in Busan from November 25, and take the lead in drafting a global plastics convention that is in line with climate goals. Please join Greenpeace in cheering Korea on as a proactive leader in the fight against plastic pollution and making important changes.
