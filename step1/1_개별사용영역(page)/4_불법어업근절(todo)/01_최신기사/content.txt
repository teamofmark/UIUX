The story of South Korea, a deep-sea powerhouse, being designated as an illegal fishing country and its escape

[작성자부분]
Written by: Taehyun Park, Marine Protection Campaigner, Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
For the past two years, Korea's deep-sea fishing industry has been in great crisis as the US and EU have designated Korea as an illegal fishing country and a potential illegal fishing country. There have been major changes, such as large-scale law revisions and reform measures for the development of the deep-sea fishing industry, until the US and EU lifted their designations as illegal fishing countries in February and April 2015, respectively. Throughout this entire process, Greenpeace has raised its voice and urged Korea to prevent illegal fishing.

#1. Illegal fishing by our country's deep-sea fishing vessels in various waters around the world is revealed

[문단 - 01]
Committing illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/419893/)
directly leads to a loss of international status. For Korea, a member of the OECD, this is not only an act that does not conform to international norms, but also an outdated act that depletes public resources that everyone uses for the short-term interests of a few. As cases of illegal fishing by Korean deep-sea fishing vessels were revealed and Korea was designated as an illegal fishing country by the United States and a potential illegal fishing country by the European Union, it clearly showed that Korea has not yet taken the lead in solving the common challenges of the 21st century.

[문단 - 02]
Illegal fishing
is a destructive fishing method that threatens the marine ecosystem, steals resources from coastal countries, and
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/431830/)
is often associated with dirty industrial practices such as human rights abuses against fishermen. There are 34 cases summarized in 
the illegal fishing report
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/multimedia/publications/2013/oceans/353504/)
published by Greenpeace in April 2013 alone. Korea's illegal fishing is not simply the fault of a few deep-sea fishing vessels, but a structural problem of the deep-sea industry that has been rampant in the world's oceans, from tuna and toothfish to migratory fish. It is even more shocking that among the companies that committed illegal fishing, there were
familiar companies
(https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/4317061/)
representing Korea .

#2. Criticism from the international community, and eventually being designated as an illegal fishing country

[문단 - 01]
South Korea, a "deep-sea power" with 342 fishing vessels
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/update/5894/blog-ocean-korea-efforts-to-clear-illegal-fishing-status/#_ftn1]
[1] operating in the world's five oceans, has taken measures that do not meet international standards as a flag state despite the numerous illegal fishing activities carried out by the deep-sea fishing industry. They have tried to cover up the situation
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/353503/]
by imposing lenient punishments that do not correspond to the scale of the crime, remaining silent ,
and even going so far as to
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2014/422521]
oppose the listing of illegal fishing vessels on the list of illegal fishing vessels in regional fisheries management organizations .

[문단 - 02]
While illegal fishing isn't unique to Korea, the South Korean government's lenient stance toward its own deep-sea fishing industry over the past several years has led many governments, including the United States, to take notice of the government's inadequate measures and the level of sanctions imposed on illegal fishing. Ultimately, in
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/429530/]
January 2013, the United States added South Korea to its list of illegal fishing nations

, along with nine others, including Colombia, Ecuador, and Ghana. In November of that year, the European Union also designated

[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/388618/]
South Korea as a potential illegal fishing nation, citing the government's inadequate control of illegal fishing .

The designation of Korea as an illegal fishing country has dealt a huge blow to Korea's status on the international stage. In particular, among the countries designated as illegal fishing countries, Korea is a country with infrastructure and economic power, and a long history of developing deep-sea industries. Therefore, the loss of national prestige due to the designation as an illegal fishing country was felt even more severely.

[문단 - 04]
The damage suffered when being designated as an illegal fishing country does not end with international embarrassment. Both the United States and the European Union have established appropriate legal systems to prevent illegal fishing, and have identified countries that do not manage and preserve regional fisheries management organizations, and have imposed
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/388618/]
sanctions such as sanctions on trade in fisheries products within the United States and the European Union and a ban on port use for all vessels from the country .
In particular, if a country is designated as an illegal fishing country by the European Union, there is the added risk of breaking existing fisheries agreements. The resulting economic losses are truly enormous.

#3. Greenpeace, working to help Korea escape illegal fishing

[문단 - 01]
The first step to solving a problem is to recognize the problem accurately. In April 2013, Greenpeace reported that illegal fishing practices by Korean deep-sea fishing vessels were inappropriate. Through this, we once again confirmed to the public and the international community that illegal fishing in Korea is a widespread practice and a problem that must be taken seriously. This was also the first step to requesting reform from the Korean government and the Korean deep-sea fishing industry.

The outdated laws and systems related to the deep-sea industry and the level of awareness that is insensitive or behind the international community’s efforts to sustainably manage marine resources are areas that desperately need reform.

#4. Finally, the revised Deep Sea Fisheries Development Act and the remaining tasks after the removal of the illegal fishing country designation 

[문단 - 01]
[버튼 - https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2015/436862/]
The Distant Water Fisheries Development Act, promulgated in January 2015, is the first step in a long journey to structurally improve the distant water fisheries industry, which still follows the same model from half a century ago.

In recognition of Korea's efforts to improve its system, the United States and the European Union decided to lift Korea's designation as an illegal fishing nation. This was because they recognized the reform of key laws related to the distant water fisheries industry and the strengthening of supervision, control, and surveillance of distant water fishing vessels as positive changes. The cooperation between the government, distant water fisheries industry, and NGOs throughout this process laid a crucial foundation for positive change. This was all the more significant because each individual worked together to address issues within their respective roles.
