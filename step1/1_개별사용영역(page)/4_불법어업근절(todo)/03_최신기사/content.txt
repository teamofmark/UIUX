Is tuna the only thing in a can of tuna?

[작성자 부분]
Written by: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
Korea is one of the world's leading tuna consumers. Koreans' love for tuna is truly special. But is tuna the only thing in the canned tuna we enjoy?

[문단 - 01]
Tuna kimchi stew, tuna fried rice, tuna tofu stew, tuna salad... These are easy to make and delicious foods that often appear on our tables. Tuna, loved by many people regardless of age and gender, is truly a magical ingredient.

[문단 - 02]
However, tuna, a food so familiar to us, has recently been attracting attention from around the world for a slightly different reason. The reasons are the destruction of the marine ecosystem due to indiscriminate overfishing and bycatch, and the horrific human rights violations occurring on ships.

[문단 - 03]
Did you know that the title of this Greenpeace series is “Is it just tuna?” This title has a special meaning. When we open a can of tuna, what we see inside is tuna meat caught in the distant sea and processed cleanly. However, behind it, there is a story about the tears of fishermen, the emptying sea, and the marine ecosystem in danger.

[부제 - 01]
Endangered Tuna Species

[문단 - 01]
Among fishermen, tuna is called the 'lawbreaker of the sea' or the 'bull of the sea'. In the case of some species, the length alone is more than 2m, which is much longer than the height of an adult male, the body weight is twice that of a cow, and the swimming speed when hunting is said to be '160km/h', which is faster than Ryu Hyun-jin's pitch or a cheetah running across the grassland.

[문단 - 02]
The tuna we know as tuna is largely classified into 7 species. There is the albacore tuna, which is popular in the United States, the yellowfin tuna, which is loved by Europeans, the skipjack tuna, which is easily available as canned tuna in Korea, the bluefin tuna (Atlantic bluefin tuna, southern bluefin tuna, Pacific bluefin tuna), which is popular as the highest quality sashimi, and lastly, the bigeye tuna, which is easily found in sashimi restaurants.

[문단 - 03]
According to the Red List of Threatened Species published by the International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources, the Atlantic bluefin tuna and southern bluefin tuna are classified as “endangered” and “critically endangered,” respectively, while the Pacific bluefin tuna and bigeye tuna are classified as “vulnerable.”

[문단 - 04]
The extinction of tuna is not simply a problem that ends with 'not being able to eat tuna'. As scholars have consistently warned, if tuna, the top predator, disappears, the balance of the food chain will be disrupted, which could lead to the destruction of the marine ecosystem.

When enough fish gather to fill the nets, the seiners set up a huge net that can be 2 km long and 200 m deep, an area the size of about 60 football fields and as high as three Ferris wheels.

[문단 - 03]
These nets catch not only young juvenile tuna and fry of rapidly declining species such as bigeye and yellowfin tuna, but also endangered sharks, rays, whales, sea turtles, dolphins, and even sea birds, as well as the entire surrounding “small ecosystem”. This indiscriminate casting of large nets and catching whatever is caught is called bycatch.

- Scrooge on the sea, longline fishing boat

[문단 - 04]
In addition to purse seiners, tuna fishing vessels include what are known as "longline vessels." While the aforementioned purse seiners and fishing gear are the primary culprits of bycatch, longline vessels specialize in overfishing. Longline vessels, which use multiple hooks attached to a single long line, can catch live fish. These vessels can have lines as long as 150 km and are equipped with over 3,000 hooks.

[부제 - 03]
The dark side of the giant global tuna company

[문단 - 01]
The problem of giant tuna companies is not only the destruction of the ecosystem due to overfishing and bycatch. They threaten the livelihood of poor island nations by dominating the market, and they are also criticized by the international community for their excessive fishing activities that ignore the human rights and safety of fishermen.
