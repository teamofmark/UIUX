The story of South Korea, a deep-sea powerhouse, being designated as an illegal fishing country and its escape
[작성자부분]
Written by Park Tae-hyun, Marine Protection Campaigner, Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
For the past two years, South Korea's distant-water fishing industry has been in deep crisis, with the US and EU designating South Korea as an IUU country and potential IUU country. The lifting of these designations by the US and EU in February and April 2015, respectively, has been a significant development, with significant legal revisions and reforms aimed at fostering the development of the distant-water fishing industry. Throughout this process, Greenpeace has consistently called on South Korea to prevent illegal fishing.

#1. Illegal fishing by Korean deep-sea fishing vessels in various waters around the world is revealed.
    [문단 - 01]
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/419893/">Engaging in illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing</a>
    directly leads to a decline in international standing. For Korea, a member of the OECD, this practice not only violates international norms but also depletes public resources shared by all for the short-term gain of a few, creating an outdated and outdated practice. The revelation of illegal fishing practices by Korean deep-sea fishing vessels, which led to the United States designating Korea as an IUU country and the European Union designating Korea as a potential IUU country, clearly demonstrates that Korea is yet to take the lead in addressing the common challenges of the 21st century.

    [문단 - 01]
    Illegal fishing is
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/431830/">a destructive fishing method that threatens marine ecosystems, steals resources from coastal countries, and</a>
    is often associated with dirty industrial practices such as human rights abuses against fishermen. Greenpeace's April 2013
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/multimedia/publications/2013/oceans/353504/">report on illegal fishing</a>
    documented 34 cases alone. Korea's illegal fishing was not simply the fault of a few deep-sea fishing vessels; it was a structural problem within the deep-sea fishing industry, which had been rampant in oceans around the world, targeting everything from tuna and toothfish to migratory fish. The fact that some of the companies involved in illegal fishing were
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/4317061/">familiar, representative Korean companies</a>
    made the situation even more shocking.

    #2. International criticism and eventual designation as an illegal fishing country.
    [문단 - 01]
    South Korea, a "deep-sea power" with 342 fishing vessels
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/update/5894/blog-ocean-korea-efforts-to-clear-illegal-fishing-status/#_ftn1">[1]</a>
    operating in the world's five oceans, has taken measures that do not meet international standards as a flag state despite the numerous illegal fishing activities carried out by the deep-sea fishing industry. They have tried to cover up the situation
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/353503/">by imposing lenient punishments that do not correspond to the scale of the crime, remaining silent</a>
    , and even going so far as to
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2014/422521/">oppose the listing of illegal fishing vessels on the list of illegal fishing vessels in regional fisheries management organizations .</a>

    [문단 - 02]
    While illegal fishing isn't unique to Korea, the South Korean government's lenient stance toward its own deep-sea fishing industry over the past several years has led many governments, including the United States, to take notice of the government's inadequate measures and the level of sanctions imposed on illegal fishing. Ultimately, in
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/429530/">January 2013, the United States added South Korea to its list of illegal fishing nations</a>
    , along with nine others, including Colombia, Ecuador, and Ghana. In November of that year, the European Union also designated
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/388618/">South Korea as a potential illegal fishing nation, citing the government's inadequate control of illegal fishing .</a>

    [이미지 - 02]
    
    [문단 - 03]
    The designation of Korea as an illegal fishing nation has dealt a significant blow to Korea's standing on the international stage. This is particularly true because, among the countries designated as illegal fishing nations, Korea possesses a robust infrastructure and economic power, as well as a long history of developing distant-water fishing industries. Therefore, the damage to Korea's national prestige caused by the designation was felt even more acutely.

    [문단 - 04]
    The damage caused by being designated as an IUU country goes beyond international embarrassment. Both the United States and the European Union have established appropriate legal systems to prevent illegal fishing, identify countries that fail to manage and conserve their regional fisheries management organizations, and impose sanctions,
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/388618/">including trade sanctions on fisheries products within the US and EU, and port bans on all vessels from those countries</a>
    . Being designated as an IUU country by the EU carries the added risk of breaching existing fisheries agreements. The resulting economic losses are truly enormous.

    [순차 목록 - 03 - 부제]
    #3. Greenpeace launches campaign to help Korea escape illegal fishing.
    [문단 - 01]
    The first step to solving a problem is to accurately recognize it. In April 2013, Greenpeace published a report exposing the inappropriate illegal fishing practices of South Korean deep-sea fishing vessels. This report reaffirmed to the public and the international community that illegal fishing in South Korea is a widespread practice and a serious problem that must be taken seriously. This also marked the beginning of a call for reform from the South Korean government and the South Korean deep-sea fishing industry.
    [문단 - 02]
    They also called for an investigation through
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2013/357013/">peaceful direct action in front of the Dongwon tuna fishing vessel Premiere, which is under investigation for illegal fishing in Liberian waters . They also</a>
    
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/2014/4297131/">held peaceful direct actions against the Insung 7 and Insung 3 fishing vessels, operated by Insung Industry, which have repeatedly violated Argentine exclusive waters, in Montevideo Port, Uruguay, and Gamcheon Port, Busan, respectively,</a>
    to demonstrate to the public that these vessels are "illegal fishing vessels."

    [이미지 - 03]
    [문단 - 03]
    Even if it were deep-sea fishing vessels that committed illegal fishing, the more fundamental problem lies in the Korean government's prioritization of industry interests over public and national interests. The outdated laws and regulations governing deep-sea fishing, coupled with a lack of awareness or insensitivity to international efforts to sustainably manage marine resources, were areas in dire need of reform.

    [문단 - 04]
    In May 2013, Greenpeace
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/feature-story/2/20131/357766/">hosted a National Assembly policy workshop with officials from various government ministries to outline the direction for Korea's distant water fisheries policy</a>
    . Since then, Greenpeace has consistently provided suggestions for institutional improvements during the two revisions to the Distant Water Fisheries Development Act. From analyzing the first revision and
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/multimedia/publications/2014/oceans/420498/">submitting reform proposals to the government</a>
    to address its shortcomings , to analyzing loopholes in the revised bill after the first revision and
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/multimedia/publications/2014/oceans/434518/">proposing a more robust version , to submitting</a>
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/multimedia/publications/2015/oceans/opinion-on-distant-water-fisheries/">opinions on revisions to subordinate statutes</a>
    to ensure the proper implementation of the Distant Water Fisheries Development Act , Greenpeace has consistently offered solutions to help Korea eradicate illegal fishing and move toward legislation that meets international standards for sustainable fisheries.

    [순차 목록 - 04 - 부제]
    #4. The Revised Distant Sea Fisheries Development Act and the Removal of Illegal Fishing Country Designations: Remaining Challenges
    [문단 - 01]
    <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/korea/news/press-release/oceans/2015/436862/">The Distant Water Fisheries Development Act, promulgated in January 2015, is the first step in a long journey to structurally improve the distant water fisheries industry, which still follows the same model from half a century ago.</a>
    In recognition of Korea's efforts to improve its system, the United States and the European Union decided to lift Korea's designation as an illegal fishing nation. This was because they recognized the reform of key laws related to the distant water fisheries industry and the strengthening of supervision, control, and surveillance of distant water fishing vessels as positive changes. The cooperation between the government, distant water fisheries industry, and NGOs throughout this process laid a crucial foundation for positive change. This was all the more significant because each individual worked together to address issues within their respective roles.
