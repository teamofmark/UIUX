Illegal fishing is destroying the marine ecosystem and our future.
[작성자부분]
Written by Kim Na-hee, Oceans Campaigner, Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
The oceans are increasingly being destroyed and emptied due to the indiscriminate and illegal fishing currently taking place worldwide. Illegal fishing activities destroy fragile marine ecosystems, indiscriminately catch marine life, putting them at risk of extinction and disrupting the ecological balance.

Will the ocean always be healthy and will there always be an unlimited supply of fish?

    [문단 - 01]
    People have long believed that the ocean is always healthy, allowing us to catch an unlimited amount of fish whenever we need them. However, this misconception has led to indiscriminate ocean pollution and the extinction of marine life like whales. To prevent the ocean from becoming desolate, "order" and "regulation" that transcend national boundaries have become necessary. Each coastal nation has established laws that meet the minimum requirements for maintaining the marine ecosystem, thereby preserving the ocean.

    [문단 - 02]
    However, fishing continues without even observing the minimum regulations necessary to sustain marine ecosystems. We call this violation of regulations "illegal fishing." Illegal fishing occurs in the form of overfishing, failure to properly report the incident, catching fish that should not be caught, or even fishing without a permit or forging documents.

    The oceans are increasingly being destroyed and emptied due to the indiscriminate and illegal fishing currently taking place worldwide. Illegal fishing activities destroy fragile marine ecosystems, indiscriminately catch marine life, putting them at risk of extinction and disrupting the ecological balance.

    [이미지 - 02]
    [문단 - 02]
    Some statistics clearly show the severity of the destruction of the marine ecosystem caused by illegal fishing. For example, it is known that the number of sharks killed for shark fin fishing is actually three to four times higher than the officially reported number. What about salmon? It is estimated that illegal catches of Russian salmon account for 60 to 90% of the legally caught and reported amount. It is said that illegal catches of Chilean tuna are five to ten times more than the legally caught and reported amount. The illegal catch of skipjack tuna, as well as the endangered bigeye and yellowfin tuna, amounts to approximately $500 million (over 500 billion won) annually.

    [문단 - 03]
    As marine ecosystems are destroyed and fish and marine life suffer, ocean productivity is also declining. If this trend continues, seafood, once a staple of our daily diet, may soon become as valuable as gold. While illegal fishing may not seem to have an immediate impact on our diets, its impact in the near future is clear.

[부제 - 03]
It makes poor countries poorer and also violates the human rights of seafarers.

    [문단 - 01]
    Illegal fishing also has a devastating impact on poor countries. Illegal fishing often occurs in waters adjacent to poor countries like Liberia, Somalia, and Sierra Leone. Indiscriminate fishing in poor countries' waters ultimately reduces the fish available to fishermen. This depletion of local marine resources, which could otherwise be harvested by local fishermen, has a devastating impact on the local economy. The global economic loss due to illegal fishing is estimated to exceed 10 trillion won, giving you an idea of ​​the scale of the problem.

    [문단 - 02]
    Human rights issues for seafarers working in the deep-sea fishing industry are a major example of illegal fishing practices. Forced to live on board ships for months in the vast ocean, seafarers often experience violence, beatings, racial discrimination, and wage exploitation.

[부제 - 04]
Join Greenpeace to stop illegal fishing, a problem in Korea!

    [문단 - 01]
    Do you understand the negative impact of illegal fishing? But what's surprising is that South Korea, known as a deep-sea power, is now being branded an illegal fishing nation. Due to illegal fishing by several deep-sea fishing companies, South Korea was designated as an illegal fishing nation by the United States in January of last year, and as a potential illegal fishing nation by the European Union in November.

    [문단 - 02]
    The most effective way to regulate illegal fishing is through law. The "Distant Sea Fisheries Development Act," currently used as the standard for deep-sea fishing, is woefully inadequate for monitoring and supervising illegal fishing. Greenpeace has announced a "Distant Sea Fisheries Policy Reform Plan" to enable the government to more effectively manage, supervise, and regulate illegal fishing in the deep-sea fishing industry, and we are continuing to communicate with the government.

    [문단 - 03]
    To change this reality, Greenpeace is recruiting "citizen police" to raise their voices in the fight against illegal fishing.
