Is there only tuna in a can of tuna?
[작성자부분]
Written by: Greenpeace Seoul Office

[문단 - 배경강조]
Korea is one of the world's leading consumers of tuna. Koreans' love for tuna is truly extraordinary. But is tuna the only thing inside the canned tuna we enjoy?

[문단 - 01]
Tuna kimchi stew, tuna fried rice, braised tuna and tofu, tuna salad... These are easy to make and delicious dishes that often appear on our tables. Tuna, beloved by people of all ages and genders, is truly a magical ingredient.

[문단 - 02]
However, tuna, a food so familiar to us, has recently been attracting global attention for a slightly different reason: the destruction of the marine ecosystem caused by indiscriminate overfishing and bycatch, and the horrific human rights abuses occurring on board ships.

[문단 - 03]
Did you know that the title of this Greenpeace series is "Is Tuna the Only Thing?" This title carries a special meaning. When we open a can of tuna, what we see inside is tuna meat caught in the distant ocean and processed cleanly. But behind the scenes, there's a story about the tears of fishermen, the emptying ocean, and the endangered marine ecosystem.

[부제 - 01]
Endangered tuna species
    [문단 - 01]
    Among fishermen, tuna are known as "the outlaws of the sea" or "the bulls of the sea." Some species can reach over two meters in length, far exceeding the height of an adult man, weighing twice as much as a cow, and swim at a staggering 160 kilometers per hour when hunting—faster than Ryu Hyun-jin's pitch or a cheetah galloping across a plain.

    [문단 - 02]
    Tuna, as we know it, is broadly categorized into seven species: albacore tuna, a favorite in the United States; yellowfin tuna, beloved by Europeans; skipjack tuna, commonly found in canned tuna in Korea; bluefin tuna (Atlantic bluefin tuna, southern bluefin tuna, Pacific bluefin tuna), popular as top-quality sashimi; and finally, bigeye tuna, commonly found in sashimi restaurants.

    [문단 - 03]
    According to the Red List of Endangered Species published by the International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources, Atlantic bluefin tuna and southern bluefin tuna are classified as “endangered” and “critically endangered,” respectively, while Pacific bluefin tuna and bigeye tuna are classified as “vulnerable.”

    [이미지 - 01]
    [문단 - 04]
    The extinction of tuna isn't simply a matter of "no longer being able to eat tuna." As scholars have consistently warned, the disappearance of tuna, a top predator, could disrupt the balance of the food chain and potentially lead to the destruction of the marine ecosystem.

[부제 - 02]

    [문단 - 03]
    When enough fish gather to fill the nets, the seiners deploy a massive net, measuring 2 kilometers long and 200 meters deep. This is equivalent to an area equivalent to 60 soccer fields and the height of three Ferris wheels.

    [문단 - 04]
    These nets catch not only juvenile skipjack tuna and the rapidly declining juvenile bigeye and yellowfin tuna, but also endangered sharks, rays, whales, sea turtles, dolphins, and even sea birds, catching the entire surrounding "small ecosystem" indiscriminately. This indiscriminate casting of large nets and catching whatever is caught is called bycatch.

    [문단 - 05]
    <strong>- Scrooge on the sea, longline fishing boat</strong>

    [문단 - 06]
    In addition to purse seiners, tuna fishing vessels include what are known as "longline vessels." While the aforementioned purse seiners and fishing gear are the primary culprits of bycatch, longline vessels specialize in overfishing. Longline vessels, which use multiple hooks attached to a single long line, can catch live fish. These vessels can have lines as long as 150 km and are equipped with over 3,000 hooks.

The Dark Side of Giant Global Tuna Companies
    [문단 - 01]
    The problem with giant tuna companies isn't just the destruction of the ecosystem caused by overfishing and bycatch. By dominating the market, they threaten the livelihoods of poor island nations. They also engage in excessive fishing practices that disregard the human rights and safety of fishermen, drawing international condemnation.
