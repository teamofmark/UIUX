    Dying white coral
    Once vibrant and colorful, coral reefs are losing their color and turning white due to rising water temperatures. Coral death not only disrupts marine habitats but also threatens the collapse of the Earth's food chain and ecosystem.

    [문단 사진 구성 - 02]
    [이미지 - 03]
    Sperm whales whose feeding activities are disturbed
    The engine propeller noise and sound waves of large ships disrupt the communication and feeding activities of marine animals such as sperm whales, threatening their survival.

    [문단 사진 구성 - 03]
    [이미지 - 04]
    The unknown world in danger, the deep sea
    If massive mining equipment were deployed in the deep sea, the seafloor would be destroyed, resulting in damage to the deep-sea ecosystem that would last for hundreds of years and could not be recovered. The deep sea is not a resource for exploitation, but a last bastion essential for human survival.

    [문단 - 01]
    In September 2025, 60 countries around the world ratified the Global Marine Protection Treaty (BBNJ), which will officially enter into force in 120 days. This is a historic milestone toward the international community's pledge to designate 30% of the high seas as marine protected areas by 2030 (30x30). Furthermore, the Seoul office produced the documentary "Signal" with the participation of approximately 3,000 citizens, leading to its theatrical release and raising awareness of the importance of ocean protection. Now, Greenpeace and citizens are demanding the following from governments:
    [비 순차 목록 - 01]

    New marine protected areas in the high seas should be designated as highly protected areas, with complete prohibition of destructive development activities and strict restrictions on human access.
